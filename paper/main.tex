\title{DFT-Validated Evidence of Ferromagnetic Mislabeling Among Dzyaloshinskii--Moriya-Candidate Materials in the Materials Project Database}

\author{Md Faiz Akhtar}
\author{Souradeep Bakshi}
\author{Rakesh Maurya}

\begin{abstract}
The Materials Project (MP) is the usual first filter in searches for
Dzyaloshinskii--Moriya-interaction (DMI) hosts, yet its workflow starts every calculation from a
ferromagnetic (FM) guess and rarely tests antiferromagnetic (AFM) alternatives. We test MP's stored
net-moment labels with density-functional theory (DFT) in the population that DMI screening selects:
noncentrosymmetric compounds containing a heavy spin--orbit element. Converging symmetry-distinct AFM
orderings for 26 candidates, we find a lower-energy AFM or ferrimagnetic
state for 21 (margins 0.19--24.68~\meVat), confirm 3 as FM, and find 2 degenerate. For two confirmed
ferromagnets, Li$_4$Fe$_2$TeWO$_{12}$ and Tb$_6$FeBi$_2$, we extract exchange and DMI ($D/J\approx3.5\%$ and
$0.7\%$) from DFT; atomistic spin dynamics with the unmodified exchange places their ordering temperatures at
$101\pm1$~K and $139\pm3$~K. MP ordering labels should be treated as a screening prior, not ground truth, for
DMI-material discovery.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Introduction}

Materials that host a stable Dzyaloshinskii--Moriya interaction (DMI)~\cite{dzyaloshinsky1958,moriya1960}
are of active interest for chiral spin-texture and skyrmion-based non-volatile memory, because the
antisymmetric exchange term the DMI contributes can stabilize spin spirals and skyrmion lattices at zero
field~\cite{bogdanov1994,fert2013,nagaosa2013}. Skyrmion lattices of this kind have been observed in bulk
chiral magnets~\cite{muhlbauer2009,yu2010}, at atomic scale in ultrathin films~\cite{heinze2011}, written and
deleted individually with a spin-polarized current~\cite{romming2013}, and stabilized at room temperature in
multilayer stacks~\cite{woo2016}; the materials landscape and the device prospects that motivate this search
are reviewed in refs.~\cite{fert2017,tokura2021}. Searching for such materials computationally typically begins
with a curated high-throughput database -- most commonly the Materials Project
(MP)~\cite{jain2013} -- used to filter tens of thousands of candidates down to a tractable shortlist before
committing expensive first-principles or simulation resources. That filtering step depends on the database's
recorded magnetic ordering (ferromagnetic, antiferromagnetic, ferrimagnetic, or non-magnetic) being correct,
or at least an unbiased estimate.

Automated high-throughput DFT workflows are prone to a specific failure mode here: relaxing a structure from
a single initial magnetic-moment guess -- almost always ferromagnetic -- converges to whichever local minimum
that guess is closest to, without necessarily finding the true magnetic ground state~\cite{horton2019}.
Materials Project's own magnetic-ordering methodology paper is explicit that a full energy-mapping analysis,
enumerating symmetry-distinct antiferromagnetic configurations and comparing converged energies, is required
to determine the ground state reliably~\cite{horton2019}, whereas entries generated at high throughput are
relaxed from a single ferromagnetic initial guess.

Recent independent evidence, obtained through a very different route, points at the same problem.
Fahmy~\cite{fahmy2026} trained propagation-vector and magnetic-order classifiers on the MAGNDATA database of
experimentally solved magnetic structures~\cite{gallego2016a,gallego2016b} and applied them to the Materials
Project, finding a systematic disagreement consistent with a ferromagnetic bias affecting of the order of
7{,}800 MP-labeled compounds. That is a large-$N$, purely statistical detection: the classifier disagrees with the
label, but no first-principles calculation is performed on any individual compound to confirm which side is
right.

Here we ask the complementary question at higher fidelity and lower $N$: within the structural family that
DMI-candidate screening pipelines actually select -- noncentrosymmetric space groups, a net magnetic moment,
and a heavy spin-orbit-coupling (SOC) element -- how often is MP's stored FM label the true DFT ground state?
We build a seven-source harmonized materials database and a structure-aware magnetic-ordering classifier, use
them to filter for DMI-compatible candidates, and run the collinear energy-mapping protocol MP itself
prescribes~\cite{horton2019} on 26 shortlisted candidates. The result is a per-compound DFT verdict rather
than a statistical disagreement: 21 of the 26 net-moment labels (24 FM, 2 ferrimagnetic) are overturned. Twenty-four of the 26 candidates, and
all three confirmed ferromagnets, are hypothetical structures generated by MP's substitution workflows, which
is itself a property of what DMI screening selects. We then carry two confirmed ferromagnets,
Li$_4$Fe$_2$TeWO$_{12}$ (mp-754090) and Tb$_6$FeBi$_2$ (mp-1079285), from DFT exchange and DMI constants to
finite-temperature atomistic spin dynamics with no fitting or rescaling, and fix the ordering temperature of
each model by finite-size scaling; for the third, FeP$_6$(WO$_8$)$_3$ (mp-850225), we resolve its two
exchange shells.

\section*{Results}

\subsection*{Screening 64,635 records to a DMI-compatible shortlist}

Seven public sources -- Materials Project~\cite{jain2013}, JARVIS-DFT~\cite{choudhary2020},
AFLOW~\cite{curtarolo2012}, MAGNDATA~\cite{gallego2016a,gallego2016b}, 2DMatPedia~\cite{zhou2019},
OQMD~\cite{saal2013,kirklin2015} and NOMAD~\cite{draxl2018} -- were merged into one schema of 64{,}635 records
(Methods). Where no ground-truth ordering exists, it is predicted by a LightGBM~\cite{ke2017} classifier on
composition, global-structure and local-magnetic-geometry features, which reaches 67.4\% accuracy (68.0\%
balanced accuracy) on a stratified held-out set of 7{,}193 records (Supplementary Fig.~\ref{figS:classifier}).
Its dominant confusion is between FM and ferrimagnetic (FiM), the two net-moment classes that the screening treats
identically. Graph neural networks trained and tested on the same split -- a crystal graph convolutional network
learned from scratch and LightGBM probes of the frozen MACE-MP-0 foundation model -- reach 63.3--74.6\% accuracy
(65.9--71.7\% balanced; Supplementary Table~\ref{tabS:gnn}, Methods). The best, which appends the MACE-MP-0
embedding to the screening features, gains 7 points of accuracy, but in agreement with Materials Project's own
labels: on the experimentally labelled MAGNDATA subset every model lies between 49 and 56\% balanced accuracy,
within the sensitivity of that small set. Retrained with
the 26 DFT-validated candidates of this work held out, the models call 7--13 of the 21 that DFT overturns
non-ferromagnetic, 1.5--2.8 times their rate on Materials Project ferromagnets. The screening below uses the
LightGBM classifier throughout.

The database is then filtered for candidates plausibly compatible with a DMI (Fig.~\ref{fig:screening}a): a
net magnetic moment (FM or FiM), a noncentrosymmetric space group (DMI is symmetry-forbidden under an
inversion centre~\cite{moriya1960}), at least one heavy-SOC element (Ta, W, Re, Os, Ir, Pt, Au, Hg, Bi -- the
classic DMI-enhancing species of the Fert--Levy and Moriya mechanisms~\cite{fert1980,moriya1960,crepieux1998,yang2015}),
and thermodynamic plausibility. Of 64{,}635 records, 47{,}614 carry a net-moment ordering, 19{,}280 of those
are noncentrosymmetric, and 1{,}864 also contain a heavy-SOC element (Materials Project 768, JARVIS 537, AFLOW
392, 2DMatPedia 77, NOMAD 53, OQMD 35, MAGNDATA 2). Dropping every record with a known hull distance above
100~\meVat\ (sources without a hull field are retained) leaves 1{,}195 (Fig.~\ref{fig:screening}b,c).
The heavy-SOC filter shifts the population sharply towards AFLOW, and the hull filter towards the sources with
no hull energy to test. After a structure-backfill and machine-learned-potential consistency check of the top
of each source's ranking (Methods), the resulting shortlist is the pool from which the DFT batch of 26
Materials Project candidates is drawn, every one carrying a confidence-1.0 net-moment label recorded by MP
(24 FM, 2 FiM). All 26 survive the
hard hull filter, and they spread from rank 1 to rank 663 of the hard-filtered ranking, so the batch is a
sample of the funnel's DMI-compatible population rather than of its most stable members (Supplementary
Fig.~\ref{figS:hullrank}).

\subsection*{DFT overturns the stored ordering of 21 of 26 candidates}

For each candidate we enumerate FM and symmetry-distinct AFM configurations and relax them with MP's own
GGA$+U$ (generalized gradient approximation plus Hubbard $U$) recipe, audit every calculation for self-consistency and for the intended per-ion spin state, and
re-decide every small margin with a same-cell (``tight'') FM/AFM pair (Methods). A label is called
overturned when an audited non-FM configuration lies more than 0.10~\meVat\ below FM -- twice the per-atom
electronic-convergence tolerance of the MP protocol -- and confirmed or degenerate only when every staged
configuration has a valid energy.

Of the 26 candidates, 21 have their FM label overturned by a lower-energy antiferromagnetic or ferrimagnetic
configuration (margins 0.19--24.68~\meVat), 3 are confirmed as the true FM ground state (1.24--2.23~\meVat\
below the next-lowest configuration), and 2 (CaLaTaFeO$_6$ with AFM 0.10~\meVat\ below FM and
Ba$_4$Ta$_{10}$FeO$_{30}$ at 0.00~\meVat, both from tight pairs) are degenerate rather than evidence for
either ordering (Fig.~\ref{fig:verdicts}a, Table~\ref{tab:summary}). No candidate remains unresolved: the one
configuration never completed (the $1\times1\times2$ stacking of mp-682554) belongs to a candidate that
another audited AFM cell already places 0.31~\meVat\ below FM. The two shortlisted candidates that MP labels
FiM (mp-22972, mp-551086) are counted against the FM alternative only, since a collinear FM/AFM enumeration
cannot represent MP's recorded ferrimagnetic configuration directly; both have a collinear configuration below
FM (an AFM cell 0.46~\meVat\ below the FM twin of the same cell for mp-22972, a ferrimagnetic Fe/Cr cell
0.80~\meVat\ below FM for mp-551086, both from tight pairs). That antiferromagnetic alignment wins this often
is unsurprising on chemical grounds: 23 of the 26 candidates are oxides, in which the magnetic cations are
bridged by oxygen -- the geometry for which Goodenough--Kanamori--Anderson superexchange between half-filled
$d$ shells favours antiparallel alignment~\cite{anderson1950,goodenough1955,kanamori1959}. A workflow that
initializes every relaxation ferromagnetically therefore starts each of these compounds from the chemically
less likely state.

The tight pairs changed four verdicts, in both directions. Zn$_2$FeWO$_6$ (mp-1047414), degenerate at
0.10~\meVat\ under the MP recipe, is AFM by 0.17--0.19~\meVat\ in both of its same-cell pairs;
Ba$_4$Ta$_{10}$FeO$_{30}$ (mp-1228547), ``confirmed FM'' by 1.18~\meVat\ under the recipe, is exactly
degenerate (0.001~\meVat) once FM and AFM are compared in the same 90-atom cell; TiFeBiPbO$_6$ (mp-1216981),
whose enumerated ``AFM'' cells had flipped only the $d^0$ Ti site, is AFM by 0.80--1.30~\meVat\ in all four
Fe-only supercells; and Tb$_6$FeBi$_2$ (mp-1079285) and FeP$_6$(WO$_8$)$_3$ (mp-850225) remain FM, with
same-cell margins on the enumerated AFM configurations (3.25 and 3.38~\meVat) larger than the recipe numbers
(2.32 and 2.09) suggested. In both compounds an ordering computed later for the exchange model sets the
final margin: the inter-chain AFM orderings of mp-1079285 lie 1.24--1.25~\meVat\ above FM, and a 68-atom cell
of mp-850225 with alternate Fe layers antiparallel along the hexagonal $c$ axis lies 1.81~\meVat\ above FM.
For mp-850225, ``FM'' describes the Fe sublattice, which is the only part the enumeration varies: its
W$^{5+}$ moments (Methods) align antiparallel to the Fe, so the DFT ground state is strictly a collinear
ferrimagnet, $2.0\,\mu_B$ per formula unit against $4.4\,\mu_B$ from Fe alone. Every overturn whose MP-recipe
margin was below 2~\meVat\ was re-decided with a tight pair in the cell of its lowest valid non-FM
configuration, and every one kept its sign, with margins moving by at most 0.32~\meVat\ (Supplementary
Table~\ref{tabS:tight}); for Li$_4$NbFe(WO$_6$)$_2$ the tight pair ($-0.47$~\meVat) lies just above a second,
recipe-only configuration ($-0.54$~\meVat), which therefore sets its margin. Fig.~\ref{fig:verdicts}a marks
which margins come from a same-cell pair.

Among the 24 candidates that resolve clearly, overturned outcomes outnumber confirmed FM outcomes 21 to 3.
The batch is drawn from the top of the DMI-filtered shortlist rather than from a random or stratified sample
of MP's labelled population, so it establishes the mislabeling rate within this structural
family rather than a database-wide estimate; it is plausible, though untested here, that this family is more
AFM-prone than the population as a whole. What it does establish is that MP's FM label cannot be taken at
face value for candidates drawn from exactly the population that DMI- and skyrmion-material screening
pipelines -- including the one used here -- select by construction.

\subsection*{Confirmed ferromagnets are hypothetical structures}

A provenance check against MP's own metadata (\texttt{theoretical} flag and Inorganic Crystal Structure Database (ICSD)~\cite{hellenbrandt2004}
cross-references, queried 2026-09-20) adds a caveat that applies to the shortlist as a whole rather than to the
ordering verdicts. Twenty-four of the 26 candidates are flagged theoretical -- structures generated by MP's
substitution and prototype workflows, 23 of them with no ICSD entry at all (mp-551086 carries an ICSD
identifier but is still flagged theoretical). Only mp-1197024 (FeBi(SeO$_3$)$_3$, DFT ground state AFM) and
mp-22972 (Tb$_6$FeBi$_2$, five ICSD entries) are experimentally reported, both overturned, and every candidate
whose FM label survives DFT is hypothetical, with MP hull distances of 0--85~\meVat
(Fig.~\ref{fig:verdicts}b). The Tb$_6$FeBi$_2$ pair makes the point directly: the experimental compound is
AFM in DFT, while the confirmed ferromagnet mp-1079285 is a hypothetical polymorph of it. The funnel's symmetry
and composition filters therefore preferentially select MP's generated structures, which is unsurprising --
noncentrosymmetric cation orderings of otherwise centrosymmetric prototypes are exactly what substitution
workflows produce -- but it means a confirmed-FM verdict here is a statement about a candidate that may not be
synthesizable, and any experimental follow-up should start from the two ICSD-backed entries.

\subsection*{First-principles exchange and DMI of two confirmed ferromagnets}

For Li$_4$Fe$_2$TeWO$_{12}$ (mp-754090, space group $P1$), confirmed FM by 2.23~\meVat, the Fe sublattice is
close-packed: each Fe has six neighbours in a triangular layer (5.11--5.16~\AA) and six across the layer
spacing (5.41--5.43~\AA) (Fig.~\ref{fig:exchange}a). The same-cell FM/AFM pair gives one effective exchange for
all twelve bonds, $J=2.78$~meV per Fe--Fe bond ($zJ=12J=33.4$~meV), with an 8\% protocol spread that we carry
as its systematic uncertainty, and a constrained noncollinear canting probe resolves one projection of the
DMI vector, $D_y=0.097$~meV per bond, so $D/J\approx3.5\%$ (Methods). This small ratio is
physically plausible:
the magnetic ion at the probed bond, Fe, is a light $3d$ species with weak intrinsic SOC, and the tungsten
that qualified this candidate for the funnel enters only through the ligand field. The presence of a heavy-SOC
element anywhere in the composition does not guarantee a large DMI at the magnetic-ion bond that anchors a
spin texture.

Tb$_6$FeBi$_2$ (mp-1079285, space group $Pm$, one Fe per primitive cell) is a different magnetic topology:
the Fe atoms form chains along $b$ (Fe--Fe 4.198~\AA), with the chain next-nearest neighbours ($\pm2b$) and
six inter-chain neighbours all at 8.40--8.41~\AA\ (Fig.~\ref{fig:exchange}b). Four same-cell pairs resolve
three exchange shells. The chain pair places the chain-antiparallel state $58.43$~meV per cell above FM,
$J_b=14.61$~meV. The two inter-chain pairs are nearly identical, $\Delta E_{2a}=22.48$ and
$\Delta E_{2c}=22.24$~meV per 18-atom cell (1.25 and 1.24~\meVat; Fe $\pm1.64$--$1.67\,\mu_B$, induced Tb
$\le0.21\,\mu_B$ in all four calculations), which supports the $J_a\approx J_c$ part of the mean-field
assumption and gives $J_\perp=2.79$~meV -- a factor of 5.2 weaker than the chain coupling, but ferromagnetic,
so the material is a three-dimensional ferromagnet of coupled FM chains rather than a quasi-one-dimensional
one. The $1\times4\times1$ $++--$ pair places that state $84.92$~meV per 36-atom cell above FM; subtracting
$4J_b$ leaves $8J_{2b}=26.50$~meV, i.e.\ $J_{2b}=3.31$~meV -- ferromagnetic and comparable to $J_\perp$, so
the $\pm2b$ bond is not a negligible correction: it carries 13\% of $zJ = 2J_b+2J_{2b}+6J_\perp = 52.6$~meV
and raises the mean-field estimate from $T_c^{\rm MF}=178$~K (chain $+$ inter-chain only) to $203$~K. By
symmetry the chain-bond DMI vector is perpendicular to $b$, and two pairs of $90^\circ$ spin spirals fix both of
its components, $\mathbf{D}\!\cdot\!\hat{n}_1=-0.074$ and $\mathbf{D}\!\cdot\!\hat{n}_2=-0.063$~meV:
$|\mathbf{D}|=0.098$~meV per chain bond and $|\mathbf{D}|/J_b=0.67\%$, five times smaller than the $D/J$ of
mp-754090, again with Fe as the only magnetic ion and the heavy elements (Tb, Bi) contributing only through
the ligand field.

$J_{2b}$ is a difference of two energies computed with different $k$-meshes, so we checked it with a
same-cell pair: the $+-+-$ chain pattern in the $1\times4\times1$ cell, which flips all four chain bonds
($\Delta E=8J_b$), gives $\Delta E=100.45$~meV per 36-atom cell, i.e.\ $J_b=12.56$~meV, 14\% below the
chain-pair value. The two pairs differ in cell shape and $k$-mesh (Methods), so the spread is the cell-shape
and $k$-convergence sensitivity of a metallic Fe chain, and it brackets the exchange model: with the
same-cell $J_b$ the residual is $J_{2b}=4.34$~meV, $zJ=50.6$~meV and $T_c^{\rm MF}=196$~K, against
$J_{2b}=3.31$, $zJ=52.6$~meV and $203$~K with the chain-pair value; $|\mathbf{D}|/J_b$ ranges over
$0.67$--$0.78\%$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:exchange}c, open markers). The production model uses the chain-pair
parameters.

The third confirmed ferromagnet, FeP$_6$(WO$_8$)$_3$ (mp-850225), has two exchange shells, six in-plane
8.77~\AA\ and six out-of-plane 8.91~\AA\ Fe--Fe bonds. A second same-cell pair, with alternate hexagonal Fe
layers antiparallel along $c$, flips every out-of-plane bond and no in-plane bond, so
$\Delta E=12J_2=123.2$~meV per 68-atom cell, giving $J_2=10.3$~meV and, with $8(J_1+J_2)=229.6$~meV from the
first pair, $J_1=18.4$~meV, both ferromagnetic. The FM members of the two pairs (the same 68 atoms in different
supercells) agree to 0.006~\meVat. Because the W$^{5+}$ moments are free to follow the Fe, these are effective
Fe--Fe couplings that include the W polarization. Its DMI could not be resolved: with one Fe per rhombohedral
cell a two-sublattice canting probe sees no DMI at all, and of the spin-spiral pair that would resolve it in
the 102-atom hexagonal cell, one chirality never reached self-consistency at the precision the extraction
needs (Methods).

\subsection*{Spin models order at 101 K and 139 K by finite-size scaling}

The DFT exchange, with no fitting or rescaling, parameterizes classical Heisenberg models of the Fe
sublattices that are run with Langevin spin dynamics in LAMMPS-SPIN~\cite{tranchida2018} from the ordered
state (Methods). Each model reproduces every DFT energy difference it was built from in a zero-step
evaluation before any sweep.

For mp-754090, $|m|$ of the 2048-spin cell decreases monotonically from 0.966 at 10~K through 0.805 at 50~K
and 0.659 at 75~K to 0.343 at 100~K, collapses to 0.068 at 125~K, and then sits on the finite-size floor
($0.025$--$0.044$ for 150--400~K; the $1/\sqrt{N}$ value for $N=2048$ is 0.022)
(Fig.~\ref{fig:spin}a, Supplementary Table~\ref{tabS:mvt754090}). An independent Metropolis Monte Carlo
calculation~\cite{metropolis1953} of the same Hamiltonian on the same lattice gives $|m| = 0.967$, $0.912$,
$0.806$, $0.661$, $0.37\pm0.03$ and $0.07\pm0.02$ at 10, 25, 50, 75, 100 and 125~K, agreeing with the
Langevin sweep to within 0.002 below 100~K and within the seed spread at 100~K, so the curve is the
equilibrium $|m|(T)$ of the model and not an artifact of the thermostat. A control sweep that adds a DMI of the
measured magnitude changes $|m|$ by at most $0.013$ (at 100~K, within the seed spread) and by $\le0.005$
elsewhere, so a 3.5\% antisymmetric term does not move the ordering scale of this model.

Finite-size scaling locates the transition. With the box aspect ratio held fixed at
$N=256$, 864, 2048 and 4000 spins, the fourth-order Binder cumulant~\cite{binder1981}
$U_4 = 1-\langle m^4\rangle/3\langle m^2\rangle^2$ of consecutive sizes crosses at $99.5$, $101.3$ and
$102.7$~K (Fig.~\ref{fig:spin}b). The crossings drift upward monotonically with system size, the expected
signature of corrections to scaling, giving $T_c = 101.2\pm1.3$~K, with the largest pair
($N=2048\times4000$, least affected by those corrections) at $102.7$~K; the susceptibility
$\chi = N(\langle m^2\rangle-\langle|m|\rangle^2)/k_BT$ peaks at 105~K independently at all four sizes. The
transition of this model is therefore the classical-Heisenberg fcc value, $k_BT_c \approx 3.18\,J$ or
$\approx103$~K~\cite{chen1993}, to within the 5~K grid spacing, while mean field ($T_c^{\rm MF} = zJ/3k_B =
129$~K) overestimates it by 28\%. The 8\% protocol spread in $J$ shifts the transition by about 8~K.

For the three-shell mp-1079285 model, $|m|$ falls smoothly from 0.988 at 5~K to 0.672 at 100~K and 0.509 at
125~K, then collapses to 0.128 at 150~K (the one point with a seed-to-seed spread, $\pm0.012$, as expected at
the transition of a finite cell) and sits on the finite-size floor of 0.03--0.05 from 200~K
(Fig.~\ref{fig:spin}c, Supplementary Table~\ref{tabS:mvt1079285}). The same-cell parametrization, swept at 10,
100, 125, 150 and 200~K, gives $|m|=0.975$, $0.668$, $0.497$, $0.124$ and $0.047$ against $0.975$, $0.672$,
$0.509$, $0.128$ and $0.048$ for the production model, so the 4\% change in $zJ$ does not move the collapse
from between 125 and 150~K. The sweep is insensitive to the DMI: $|\mathbf{D}|/J_b=0.67\%$ produces a canting
angle of order $D/2J\approx0.2^\circ$ per bond, far below the thermal disorder at any temperature on the grid.

The $U_4(T)$ curves of consecutive sizes cross at $140.9$, $141.4$ and $135.6$~K, giving
$T_c = 139.3\pm2.6$~K, and $\chi$ peaks at 145~K for the three smaller cells and 140~K for the largest
(Fig.~\ref{fig:spin}d). This puts the transition at $0.68\,T_c^{\rm MF}$, just below the isotropic
simple-cubic ratio $0.72$ (0.79 for fcc~\cite{chen1993}), as the exchange anisotropy leads one to expect. The
crossings are not monotonic in system size as they are for mp-754090, and the analysis is harder here: the
exchange is strongly anisotropic ($J_b/J_\perp = 5.2$, though ferromagnetic on every shell), so intra-chain
correlations survive well above the three-dimensional ordering temperature and the $U_4$ curves separate
gradually rather than pivoting about a point, and the fluctuations near $T_c$ are correspondingly larger. The
135--150~K window was therefore run with 10--14 seeds per point, six of them 80~ps; the
three crossings then agree to $\pm2.6$~K. Both spreads over pairwise crossings are lower bounds on the uncertainty
of the model $T_c$: re-deriving the crossings under changes to the sampling window shows that the
autocorrelation time of $U_4$ near $T_c$ is comparable to the run length (Methods), and the DFT coupling
uncertainties come on top.

\section*{Discussion}

Fahmy's classifier~\cite{fahmy2026} detects the Materials Project's ferromagnetic bias statistically, at
large $N$; the present work reaches the same conclusion by the opposite route, a small-$N$ DFT energy-mapping
check, in exactly the noncentrosymmetric, heavy-SOC-element, net-moment slice that DMI- and skyrmion-material
pipelines target. A 21-of-26 overturn rate in that slice is a direct warning to any workflow, including our
own, that uses MP's ordering label as a hard screening criterion rather than a prior.

The consequence runs in both directions. The funnel used here excludes AFM-labeled candidates, on the reasoning
that a net moment is required for the texture-hosting mechanisms it targets. If 21 of 26 top-ranked
FM-labeled candidates are in fact AFM or ferrimagnetic, downstream compute -- structure backfill, potential
relaxation, spin dynamics -- risks being spent on candidates whose net-moment premise is wrong, while part of
the true net-moment landscape may be filtered out before any calculation is spent on it. The corrective is not
to trust a classifier over MP either -- both are estimates -- but to treat any ordering field,
database-recorded or predicted, as a prior to be checked with DFT before committing expensive downstream
stages. The provenance result adds a second, independent prior: in this family the screening preferentially
returns hypothetical structures (24 of 26 here, and all three confirmed ferromagnets), so a verified magnetic
ground state still has to be paired with a synthesizability assessment before it is an experimental target.

For two confirmed candidates, the full chain from DFT to finite temperature costs one and five same-cell pairs
and two and four constrained noncollinear calculations, respectively, plus a finite-size-scaling campaign, and returns ordering
temperatures of the models, $101\pm1$~K and $139\pm3$~K, with no adjustable parameter. Both DMI-to-exchange
ratios are a few percent or less, despite the heavy elements that qualified the compounds for screening, which
argues for adding a magnetic-ion-resolved SOC criterion to DMI screening rather than an element-presence one.

Several limitations bound these conclusions. The 26-candidate batch is drawn from the top of one
DMI-filtered shortlist, not a random sample of MP's labelled population (1{,}864 candidates pass
the DMI funnel, 1{,}195 with the hull as a hard filter), so the mislabeling rate is a property of this
structural family. The verdict rule is deliberately asymmetric, and the 12 overturns decided on an MP-recipe
pair alone carry a protocol uncertainty that the sixteen doubly-computed configurations bound at 1.3~\meVat;
only one of them has $|$margin$|<2$~\meVat\ (Li$_4$NbFe(WO$_6$)$_2$, $-0.54$), and a tight pair on a second
configuration corroborates its sign ($-0.47$~\meVat). The mp-754090 DMI probe resolves one projection of what
is, in $P1$ symmetry, eight unconstrained three-component DMI vectors, so its spin model is exchange-only with
the DMI bounded by a control; the DMI of mp-850225 is not resolved. The spin models are classical, unit-spin,
rigid-lattice models with zero single-ion anisotropy, which was not probed in any compound; the mp-754090
model assigns one $J$ to two geometrically distinct shells, the mp-1079285 model one $J_\perp$ to six
inter-chain bonds, and Tb is treated with its $4f$ electrons in the core, so any Tb--Fe exchange is absent.
The Binder-cumulant crossings are therefore critical temperatures of the spin models, not of the materials:
they inherit an 8\% spread in $J$ for mp-754090 and a 14\% spread in $J_b$ for mp-1079285 on top of the
classical treatment of the moment.

Within the structural family that DMI-candidate screening selects, MP's ferromagnetic label is wrong more often
than it is right. High-throughput ordering labels should be used as a screening prior rather than ground truth,
particularly in the structural families that a downstream physical mechanism -- here, the DMI -- selects for.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Harmonized multi-source database}

Materials Project~\cite{jain2013} (37{,}783 records, via the \texttt{mp-api} REST
interface~\cite{ong2015}), JARVIS-DFT~\cite{choudhary2020} (19{,}116), AFLOW~\cite{curtarolo2012} (2{,}639),
MAGNDATA~\cite{gallego2016a,gallego2016b} (1{,}919 of 2{,}414 attempted), 2DMatPedia~\cite{zhou2019}
(1{,}862), OQMD~\cite{saal2013,kirklin2015} (816), and NOMAD~\cite{draxl2018} (500) were merged into one
schema (\texttt{material\_id}, \texttt{formula}, \texttt{elements}, \texttt{space\_group}, \texttt{density},
\texttt{volume}, \texttt{band\_gap}, \texttt{ordering}, \texttt{total\_magnetization}, and, where available,
full structure and per-site magnetic-moment vectors), for 64{,}635 total records. Records retain a source tag
rather than being silently deduplicated across sources. MAGNDATA is the only source with experimentally
determined (rather than DFT-derived) magnetic structure, and is accordingly the ground-truth source for
classifier training and validation. A scraper against the live Bilbao Crystallographic Server index recovered 1{,}919 of 2{,}414 entries
(79.5\%); the failures, mostly transient server errors, are analysed in Supplementary Note~1.
Ordering was validated on a known case (LaMnO$_3$, correctly recovered as AFM, space group $Pnma$).

\subsection*{Structure-aware magnetic-ordering classifier}

A LightGBM~\cite{ke2017} gradient-boosted-decision-tree classifier predicts ordering (FM, AFM, FiM or nonmagnetic, NM) from
composition- and structure-derived features, in the spirit of general-purpose composition-based property
models~\cite{ward2016,schmidt2019} and of earlier machine-learning treatments of magnetic order and ordering
temperature~\cite{nelson2019,court2020,frey2020,kabiraj2020}. A composition-only baseline (per-element
statistics of atomic number, mass, electronegativity, atomic radius, plus group, period, transition-metal and rare-earth
fractions) achieves 56.7\% accuracy (60.7\% balanced accuracy). Adding two tiers of structural features --
broad, near-universal-coverage fields (space group number, crystal-system bucket, unit-cell volume, density,
site count) and local-geometry features around magnetic sites (nearest magnetic--magnetic neighbour distance,
magnetic coordination number, fraction of magnetic sites; computed from atomic positions and species only,
never from the magnetic-moment vectors that generate the label itself) -- raises this to 67.4\% accuracy
(68.0\% balanced accuracy) on a stratified 28{,}769/7{,}193 train/test split (Supplementary
Fig.~\ref{figS:classifier}). The local-geometry tier is computed for every source with a public structure
endpoint (Materials Project 37{,}783/37{,}783, JARVIS 19{,}116/19{,}116, AFLOW 2{,}637/2{,}639, NOMAD
495/500, 2DMatPedia 1{,}862/1{,}862; OQMD 0/816 because its REST endpoint was unreachable). Computing it for
MAGNDATA alone, whose CIFs carry atomic positions inline, would make ``tier present'' a proxy for the one
source whose labels are experimental and 83\% AFM; with full coverage the headline numbers are essentially
unchanged (67.0\% $\to$ 67.4\% accuracy, 68.1\% $\to$ 68.0\% balanced), but per-source test accuracy on
Materials Project rises from 65.6\% to 66.9\% (balanced 63.0\% $\to$ 65.9\%), the inflated MAGNDATA figure
drops from 89.5\% to 75.8\%, and four local-geometry features (mean/std/min magnetic--magnetic distance, mean
magnetic coordination) enter the top seven features by LightGBM split importance, where before none appeared
in the top ten. Space-group number remains the single most important feature, despite being absent from the
baseline entirely. The classifier is retrained on all labeled data and deployed to predict ordering for every
record lacking a ground-truth label, including all 19{,}116 JARVIS records, none of which carry a populated
ordering field.

\subsection*{Graph neural network classifiers}

Two graph-based models were trained on the train/test split of the LightGBM classifier and scored, together with
it, on the 7{,}161 of its 7{,}193 test records whose structure is fully ordered (the other 32 are partially
occupied MAGNDATA structures). Each crystal is a periodic graph whose nodes are atoms and whose edges join every
atom to its 12 nearest neighbours within 8~\AA, built from species, positions and lattice only; no magnetic
moment enters, since the MAGNDATA labels are derived from the moments. The first is a crystal graph
convolutional neural network (CGCNN)~\cite{xie2018} trained from scratch in PyTorch Geometric~\cite{fey2019}: a
learned 64-dimensional element embedding projected to 128 channels, four gated graph convolutions with batch
normalization and residual updates, bond lengths expanded in 40 Gaussians over 0--8~\AA, mean pooling over atoms
and a two-layer head. It is trained with class-weighted cross-entropy and AdamW (learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight
decay $10^{-4}$, cosine schedule) for 120 epochs in batches of 128, keeping the epoch of best balanced accuracy on
a stratified 10\% validation fraction of the training set, with three seeds on one 24~GB GPU. The second probes a
pretrained foundation potential: the invariant per-atom descriptors of both interaction layers of the frozen
MACE-MP-0 \texttt{medium} model~\cite{batatia2023} (128 channels, \texttt{max\_L}$=1$, $r_\mathrm{max}=6.0$~\AA;
distinct from the fine-tuned \texttt{small} model used for relaxations below) are pooled over atoms by mean and
standard deviation into a 512-dimensional vector, which LightGBM, with the settings of the screening classifier,
classifies alone and concatenated with the screening features. MACE-MP-0 is trained on energies, forces and
stresses only, so the embedding carries no ordering label, but it is not independent of Materials Project: its
MPtrj training set holds relaxation trajectories of the same structures, computed in their stored magnetic
orderings. Accuracy on the Materials Project rows measures agreement with Materials Project's label, which is
the label this work finds biased towards FM; the MAGNDATA rows (311 AFM, 39 FiM, 19 FM in the test set) are the
only experimental ground truth, and their balanced accuracy shifts by up to 4 points when the training set changes by
only the 26 candidates and by 6 points between CGCNN seeds, as much as it differs between models. For an external test against DFT, every model was
retrained with the 26 DFT-validated candidates removed from training, and its predictions for them compared with
the DFT verdicts; with 21 overturned and 3 confirmed candidates these are reported as counts.

\subsection*{Screening funnel and shortlist}

The noncentrosymmetric filter was verified computationally against all 230 space groups, recovering the
textbook 92 centrosymmetric / 138 noncentrosymmetric split. A hull energy is known for 1{,}340 of the
1{,}864 heavy-SOC survivors (the MP, JARVIS and OQMD records), 671 of which lie within 100~\meVat\ of
it. The \texttt{ehull} field of the JARVIS-DFT snapshot used here is not a hull distance (Supplementary
Note~2), so the hull distance of every JARVIS record was recomputed from JARVIS's own OptB88vdW formation
energies with a per-chemical-system convex hull (13{,}967 of 19{,}116 records within 100~\meVat); three of the
four JARVIS entries in the original top-30 shortlist fail this criterion, and none entered the DFT batch,
which is Materials Project only. The hull criterion is available both as a hard filter (records from sources that provide no
hull energy -- AFLOW, NOMAD, 2DMatPedia, MAGNDATA -- are retained rather than penalised for a missing field;
1{,}195 candidates) and as a sort key; the DFT batch was drawn from the sort-key ranking, which is kept
alongside so the batch remains reproducible.

For the top 100 screened candidates per source, structures were backfilled via each source's own public
data-access method (298 of 300 attempted backfills succeeded; two AFLOW records failed on repeated TLS errors
from the AFLUX endpoint) and re-relaxed with a MACE foundation-model potential~\cite{batatia2022,batatia2023},
fine-tuned on the harmonized dataset via multihead training (a replay head sampling 20{,}000 configurations
from the original pretraining distribution to avoid catastrophic forgetting), as an independent consistency
check against each source's own DFT relaxation. The base model is MACE-MP-0 \texttt{small}
(\texttt{num\_channels}$=128$, \texttt{max\_L}$=0$, two interaction layers, $r_\mathrm{max}=6.0$~\AA); the
relaxations use the Stage-2 stochastic-weight-averaging checkpoint of the fine-tune with its default head, and are static
FIRE~\cite{bitzek2006} optimizations in ASE~\cite{larsen2017}. Other universal potentials trained on Materials
Project data, such as M3GNet~\cite{chen2022} and CHGNet~\cite{deng2023}, would serve the same purpose. All 298
relaxations converged (max force $\le 0.05$~eV~\AA$^{-1}$) with a median relaxation-energy shift of
0.9~\meVat\ relative to the source structure. Classifier confidence and MACE relaxation consistency
were merged into a final ranking (converged $\to$ hull-stable $\to$ smallest relaxation shift $\to$ classifier
confidence); the resulting top-30 shortlist is the pool the DFT batch is drawn from.

\subsection*{DFT ordering-validation protocol}

For shortlisted candidates carrying an \path{ordering_source=ground_truth}, \path{confidence=1.0} label
from Materials Project (i.e., MP's own recorded ordering, not a classifier prediction), we apply the
collinear magnetic-ordering energy-mapping approach behind MP's own workflow~\cite{horton2019}:
symmetry-distinct FM and AFM spin configurations are enumerated with
\path{pymatgen.MagneticStructureEnumerator}~\cite{ong2013} (backed by \texttt{enumlib}); VASP inputs are
generated with \texttt{MPRelaxSet} -- MP's own PBE exchange-correlation functional~\cite{perdew1996}, GGA+U
parameters~\cite{dudarev1998,wang2006,jain2011} (e.g. LDAUU$=5.3$~eV on Fe), plane-wave cutoff (520~eV), and
Monkhorst--Pack $k$-point convention~\cite{monkhorst1976} -- overriding only the initial MAGMOM per
configuration; and each configuration is relaxed to convergence within Kohn--Sham density-functional
theory~\cite{hohenberg1964,kohn1965} as implemented in VASP~6.4.3~\cite{kresse1996} with the
projector-augmented-wave method~\cite{blochl1994,kresse1999} (\texttt{MPRelaxSet} defaults ISIF$=3$,
ISMEAR$=-5$, i.e.\ the tetrahedron method with Bl\"ochl corrections~\cite{blochl1994tet},
EDIFF$=5\times10^{-5}$~eV per atom, i.e.\ 0.05~\meVat). This keeps the energies directly comparable to MP's
stored values rather than constituting an independent DFT recipe. Beyond VASP's own convergence flag, every
calculation was audited in two further ways: the final RMS charge-density residual was checked (the ``EDIFF is
reached'' banner can fire on a transient while the density is still far from self-consistent), and the final
per-ion moments were compared against the intended MAGMOM pattern and against the moment magnitudes of the FM
calculation of the same compound. A configuration whose magnetic ions collapse to a low-spin or nonmagnetic
state, or whose moments re-order into the FM pattern during the self-consistent-field (SCF) cycle (a doubled ``AFM'' cell that is really
FM), passes VASP's convergence test but is not a sample of the ordering it was labeled with, and is excluded
from the verdict rather than counted. As a check of the setup, the converged FM configuration of mp-861559
(TaFeO$_4$) reproduces MP's recorded total magnetization to within $0.003\,\mu_B$ (30.00000 vs.\
$30.0022\,\mu_B$ for 36 atoms), ruling out a convention mismatch, while three symmetry-distinct AFM
configurations all converge below it, the lowest by 0.274~eV per cell ($\approx$45.7~meV per Fe).

The first pass (93 relaxations across the 26 candidates; 116 audited relaxations in total once the second
batch is included) left 12 calculations in seven candidates that failed the spin-state audit: collapsed or
low-spin Fe/Cr moments in an AFM configuration (mp-1173139, mp-1173143, mp-551086, mp-775194), ``AFM'' cells
whose moments re-ordered to the FM pattern during the SCF cycle (mp-682554), an ``AFM'' pattern that had
flipped only a nonmagnetic $d^0$ Ti site and was therefore FM in disguise (mp-1216981), and an FM reference
that collapsed to a nonmagnetic state (mp-22972). A second batch replaced every failed configuration with a
freshly enumerated one in a spin state that the audit accepts: additional symmetry-distinct AFM/FiM cells for
mp-1173139, mp-1173143, mp-775194 and mp-551086 (the ferrimagnetic ones with the two Fe sublattices, or Fe and
Cr, unequal in number), Fe-only AFM supercells for mp-1216981 (Ti excluded from the enumeration),
anti-collapse FM reruns for mp-22972 (in the primitive cell and in the cell of its lowest AFM configuration),
and, for mp-682554, whose $1\times1\times2$ Fe--Fe stacking (130 atoms, 27.9~\AA\ between the two Fe sites)
diverged twice at the first density-mixing step through charge sloshing, five single-point mixer/smearing
variants of the same cell run side by side, with the production relaxation restarted only under the first
variant that converged; that production run was stopped before completion and is the one configuration
missing from the batch. A superseded attempt (a diverged or collapsed run that was re-done) is neither a sample
nor a gap in the count.

Two protocol effects matter at the sub-meV~atom$^{-1}$ level. First, the MP recipe compares an FM relaxation
in the primitive cell with AFM relaxations in supercells, each with its own $k$-mesh and each relaxed with
ISIF$=3$ to its own cell shape; for the sixteen configurations run both ways this ``loose'' margin differs from
a ``tight'' one (both orderings relaxed with ISIF$=2$ in the same relaxed AFM cell, identical $k$-mesh,
EDIFF$=10^{-6}$~eV, EDIFFG$=-0.01$~eV~\AA$^{-1}$) by up to 1.3~\meVat\ in either direction
(Ba$_4$Ta$_{10}$FeO$_{30}$: $+1.18\to0.00$; FeP$_6$(WO$_8$)$_3$: $+2.09\to+3.38$). Every candidate whose
verdict rested on a loose margin smaller than about 2~\meVat\ was therefore re-decided with a tight pair built
in the cell of its lowest valid non-FM configuration (14 pairs in the first tight batch, 6 more for the
remaining small-margin overturns), and a tight number replaces the loose one for that configuration wherever
it exists. Second, the verdict rules are asymmetric by construction: a label is \emph{overturned} as soon as
one audited configuration lies more than 0.10~\meVat\ below FM -- a still-missing configuration cannot undo
that -- whereas \emph{confirmed FM} and \emph{degenerate} require every staged configuration to have a valid
energy. The 0.10~\meVat\ window is twice the per-atom EDIFF of the loose protocol. The two 130-atom tight
relaxations of mp-682554, first cut off short of the force criterion, were continued to convergence (29 and
32 ionic steps, final maximum forces 0.006 and 0.010~eV~\AA$^{-1}$); the gap moved from 0.315 to
0.313~\meVat\ (40.7~meV per cell).

All five tungsten-bearing candidates initially failed to converge under MP's standard Hubbard correction
(LDAUU$=6.2$~eV on W): tungsten in these oxides is typically W$^{6+}$, a nominally $d^0$ ion, and a large $U$
applied to a near-empty $d$ shell leaves the self-consistent density with no restoring force, oscillating
indefinitely rather than converging. Removing $U$ from tungsten (LDAUL$=-1$), applied uniformly across every
ordering of the affected compound, converged all 18 corresponding production calculations on the first retry.
One of the five is not $d^0$: in FeP$_6$(WO$_8$)$_3$ (mp-850225), charge balance with Fe$^{3+}$ and P$^{5+}$
requires W$^{5+}$ ($d^1$), and in the U-free runs each W carries 0.78--0.86~$\mu_B$ antiparallel to Fe in the
FM state. These reruns also fix the cell shape and volume at MP's relaxed values (ISIF$=2$, ions only) and use
Gaussian smearing (ISMEAR$=0$, $\sigma=0.05$~eV; tetrahedron smearing for mp-755203) for SCF stability. This
changes the absolute energy scale for those five candidates relative to MP's U-inclusive value, but not the
FM-versus-AFM comparison within each compound, since the same settings are applied to every ordering of that
compound.

\subsection*{Exchange and DMI of mp-754090}

Effective spin-Hamiltonian parameters are extracted by energy mapping, the standard route from the total
energies of constructed spin configurations to Heisenberg
parameters~\cite{liechtenstein1987,xiang2011,xiang2013}. The isotropic exchange is obtained from the tight
same-cell FM/AFM pair used for the ordering verdict (both orderings relaxed in the same cell, which is the
fixed lattice a Heisenberg $J$ is defined on). Each of the two Fe sites (Fe0, Fe1) per primitive cell of the
rock-salt-derived Li$_4$Fe$_2$TeWO$_{12}$ structure has twelve Fe neighbours, six in a triangular layer
(5.112--5.157~\AA, bond angles of $60^\circ$, $120^\circ$ and $180^\circ$; four on the opposite sublattice and
two on the same sublattice by lattice translation) and six across the 4.53~\AA\ layer spacing
(5.411--5.427~\AA; again four on the opposite sublattice and two on the same). The next shell starts at
7.45~\AA. For $H=-\sum_{\langle ij\rangle} J\,\hat{s}_i\!\cdot\!\hat{s}_j$ with each bond counted once, the
collinear AFM configuration (Fe0 sublattice reversed) flips the eight inter-sublattice bonds per cell -- four
in-plane and four inter-layer -- so $E_\mathrm{AFM}-E_\mathrm{FM} = 2\times8\,J = 16J$; with the same-cell DFT
value $\Delta E = 44.54$~meV per two-Fe cell this gives $J = \Delta E/16 = 2.78$~meV per bond. The loose pair
(each ordering at its own ISIF$=3$ optimum) gives $\Delta E = 48.23$~meV and $J=3.01$~meV, an 8\% protocol
spread that we carry as the systematic uncertainty of $J$. A single FM/AFM pair cannot separate the in-plane
from the inter-layer exchange, nor resolve the four same-sublattice bonds, which are invisible to a
two-configuration fit; all twelve bonds are assigned the same $J$ in the spin model -- an explicit
assumption. This is a single-$J$ effective model, not a multi-shell decomposition; its $zJ = 12J = 33.4$~meV,
which fixes the mean-field ordering temperature, is what the DFT pair determines directly.

DMI does not appear in a collinear FM-versus-AFM comparison; it requires spins canted relative to one another.
The Li/Fe/Te/W cation ordering reduces the space group to $P1$, so no inversion centre at a bond midpoint
forbids it~\cite{moriya1960}. We ran two static, constrained noncollinear-spin+SOC calculations from the
relaxed FM structure -- the established route to a first-principles DMI~\cite{heide2008,freimuth2014} --
within unconstrained noncollinear PAW magnetism~\cite{hobbs2000}, using the penalty-energy
method~\cite{ma2015} (\texttt{I\_CONSTRAINED\_M=1}, constraining moment direction only) to hold Fe0 along
$+\hat{z}$ and Fe1 along $+\hat{x}$ (canted$_+$) or $-\hat{x}$ (canted$_-$) -- i.e. $\theta=\pm 90^\circ$
canting in the $xz$-plane, an arbitrary but fixed reference axis since $P1$ symmetry privileges no plane. The
constraint held essentially exactly (Fe1 converged to $4.038\,\mu_B$ along $x$ in canted$_+$ and
$-4.039\,\mu_B$ in canted$_-$; Fe0 leakage off $z$ was $<0.002\,\mu_B$ in both). With the same bond-counting
convention as for $J$ ($z=8$ inter-sublattice bonds per cell, each counted once; the four same-sublattice
bonds connect parallel spins and contribute to neither term), $E(\theta) = E_0 - zJ\cos\theta - zD\sin\theta$,
and comparing $\theta=\pm90^\circ$ isolates the antisymmetric term since $\cos(\pm90^\circ)=0$:
\begin{equation}
zD = \frac{E(\theta{=}-90^\circ) - E(\theta{=}+90^\circ)}{2},
\end{equation}
giving $E(-90^\circ)-E(+90^\circ)=1.546$~meV per cell and $D = 0.097$~meV per bond, i.e.\ $D/J \approx 3.5\%$.
Because $\hat{s}_1=\hat{z}$ and the canting is confined to the $xz$-plane,
$\hat{s}_1\times\hat{s}_2 = (0,\sin\theta,0)$: this probe measures the mean $y$-component of the DMI vectors of
the eight inequivalent inter-sublattice bonds in this lab frame, not a generic magnitude, and is a resolved
DFT number for one projection of $D$ rather than a fully characterized three-dimensional DMI vector; a
complete characterization would require at least eight independent canting probes.

\subsection*{Exchange and DMI of mp-1079285}

The Fe atoms of Tb$_6$FeBi$_2$ form chains along $b$ (Fe--Fe 4.198~\AA), the next Fe--Fe distances being
8.396~\AA\ along $\pm2b$ and 8.396--8.407~\AA\ to the six chain neighbours along $\pm a$, $\pm c$, $\pm(a+c)$.
Tb is treated with the $4f$ electrons frozen in the core (\texttt{Tb\_3} PAW), so the only moment in the model
is Fe ($1.68\,\mu_B$ in the FM calculation), an explicit approximation that ignores any Tb--Fe exchange. Every
collinear AFM configuration that the enumeration produced for this candidate turned out to be the same
chain-antiparallel order in a different cell setting, so the tight pair in the $1\times2\times1$ cell resolves
only the intra-chain coupling: with $H=-\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}J_{ij}\,\hat{s}_i\!\cdot\!\hat{s}_j$ (each bond
counted once) it flips two chain bonds per cell, $E_\mathrm{AFM}-E_\mathrm{FM}=4J_b$. A chain model alone has
no order at any $T>0$, so three further same-cell tight pairs were staged in supercells of the relaxed FM cell
(\texttt{ISIF}$=2$, \texttt{EDIFF}$=10^{-6}$~eV, \texttt{EDIFFG}$=-0.01$~eV~\AA$^{-1}$): the $2a$ and $2c$
cells with the two Fe antiparallel, which flip the $\pm a,\pm(a+c)$ and the $\pm c,\pm(a+c)$ bonds
respectively ($\Delta E_{2a}=4(J_a+J_{ac})$, $\Delta E_{2c}=4(J_c+J_{ac})$), and a $1\times4\times1$ cell with
the chain pattern $++--$, which flips two of the four chain bonds and all four $\pm2b$ bonds
($\Delta E_{4b}=4J_b+8J_{2b}$). Two equations do not determine three inter-chain couplings; the model takes
their mean, $J_\perp=(\Delta E_{2a}+\Delta E_{2c})/16$, under the stated assumption $J_a\approx J_c\approx
J_{ac}$ (the three bond lengths agree to 0.01~\AA). The $\pm2b$ bond is resolved separately because its length
coincides with the inter-chain shell, which no radial exchange function in the spin-dynamics code can
separate. The $J_b$ and $\Delta E_{4b}$ pairs use different $k$-meshes ($3\times3\times3$ on the
$1\times2\times1$ cell, $3\times2\times3$ on the $1\times4\times1$ cell) and cells: the $1\times2\times1$ pair
sits in the relaxed cell of the enumerated AFM configuration ($a=8.372$, $c=8.376$~\AA) with a mesh equivalent
to 6 points along $b$ in the primitive cell, the $1\times4\times1$ pairs in the relaxed FM cell ($a=8.407$,
$c=8.396$~\AA) with 8. The same-cell $+-+-$ check is compared with the FM member of the $++--$ pair; its
relaxation, first cut off at ionic step 24, was continued to the force criterion (28 ionic steps, final
maximum force 0.007~eV~\AA$^{-1}$), lowering $\Delta E$ by 0.48~meV from the cut-off value.

The $Pm$ mirror plane is normal to $b$ and contains the Fe site, so by Moriya's rules $\mathbf{D}\perp b$ and
two projections determine $|\mathbf{D}|$. Each was obtained from a pair of $90^\circ$ spin spirals in the
$1\times4\times1$ cell (four Fe, moments constrained with \texttt{I\_CONSTRAINED\_M}$=1$, $\lambda=10$, SOC on,
\texttt{EDIFF}$=10^{-7}$~eV, single points on the relaxed FM structure), rotating in the plane normal to
$\hat{n}_1\parallel a$ or $\hat{n}_2=\hat{b}\times\hat{n}_1$ with either chirality. For a $90^\circ$ spiral
$\hat{s}_i\times\hat{s}_j=\pm\hat{n}$ on every chain bond while the symmetric exchange (including $J_{2b}$,
whose bonds are antiparallel in both chiralities) is identical for the two, so
$\mathbf{D}\!\cdot\!\hat{n}=[E(+)-E(-)]/8$ per bond. The constraint penalty energies were
$\le1.6\times10^{-9}$~eV, the Fe moments had no component off their target axes (to the printed
$0.001\,\mu_B$) with $|m_\mathrm{Fe}|=1.50\pm0.01\,\mu_B$ in all four calculations, and the induced Tb moments
($\le0.123\,\mu_B$; Bi $\le0.002\,\mu_B$) rotate with the spiral, their magnitudes agreeing between
chiralities to $0.001\,\mu_B$.

\subsection*{Exchange of mp-850225 and the unresolved spiral}

FeP$_6$(WO$_8$)$_3$ has one Fe per rhombohedral cell, so its Fe sublattice is a Bravais lattice on which a
two-sublattice canting probe sees no DMI (the $+\mathbf{t}$ and $-\mathbf{t}$ bonds contribute opposite
$\mathbf{D}\cdot(\mathbf{S}_0\times\mathbf{S}_1)$). The two exchange shells are separated by two same-cell
pairs in 68-atom supercells: the first, from the ordering enumeration, gives $\Delta E=8(J_1+J_2)$; the
second, with alternate hexagonal Fe layers antiparallel along $c$, every
out-of-plane bond and no in-plane one ($\Delta E=12J_2$). Resolving $D$ needs a spin spiral in the 102-atom
hexagonal cell. That spiral pair (noncollinear with SOC, Fe directions constrained, W moments free) was run in
three attempts (about 65 node-hours): one chirality converged ($\mathrm{EDIFF}=10^{-6}$~eV), but the other
never reached self-consistency at the precision the extraction needs ($\Delta E=18\sin120^\circ
D_z\approx15.6\,D_z$, i.e.\ about 1.6~meV for $D\sim0.1$~meV), so no DMI is quoted for this compound.

\subsection*{Finite-temperature spin dynamics}

The DFT-derived $J=2.78$~meV, with no fitting or rescaling, parameterizes a twelve-neighbour Heisenberg
Hamiltonian for the Fe sublattice of mp-754090 (framework Li/Te/W/O sites carry no spin and are omitted from
the geometry), implemented in LAMMPS-SPIN~\cite{tranchida2018} within
LAMMPS~\cite{plimpton1995,thompson2022} via the \path{spin/exchange} pair style (which counts each bond once,
matching the convention of the DFT extraction) with a 5.5~\AA\ cutoff that captures all twelve Fe--Fe bonds
per site. \path{spin/exchange} uses the Bethe--Slater radial form $J(r)=4a\,(r/d)^2 e^{-(r/d)^2}$ (with its
$b$ parameter set to zero); $d=5.274$~\AA\ is solved so that $J(5.130~\text{\AA})=J(5.418~\text{\AA})$
exactly and $a$ set so that both equal the DFT $J$, which makes $J(r)$ constant to within 0.06\% over the two
shells. A zero-step energy evaluation of the FM and Fe0-reversed configurations of the model reproduces the
DFT $E_\mathrm{AFM}-E_\mathrm{FM}=16J=44.54$~meV per cell to 0.003\%. The DMI is not included in the production
sweep: the LAMMPS \path{spin/dmi} pair style implements $\mathbf{D}_{ij} = D\,(\hat{e}_{ij}\times\hat{v})$
with a single global vector $\hat{v}$, and for the DFT probe geometry ($\hat{s}_i\times\hat{s}_j=\pm\hat{y}$)
its energy difference is $D\,(\hat{v}\times\hat{y})\cdot\sum_b\hat{e}_b$ over the eight inter-sublattice bond
directions -- a sum that is 0.03 (in units of a bond vector) for this near-centrosymmetric neighbour shell, so
no choice of $\hat{v}$ can reproduce the DFT $E(-90^\circ)-E(+90^\circ)$ (a zero-step evaluation with
$D=0.097$~meV and $\hat{v}=\hat{y}$ gives $0.000$~meV); a faithful model needs the eight bond-resolved DMI
vectors. We instead bound the effect of a DMI of the measured magnitude on $|m|(T)$ with a control sweep that
adds \path{spin/dmi} with $D=0.097$~meV and $\hat{v}=\hat{y}$ (an arbitrary but representative placement of
a 3.5\% antisymmetric term) at four temperatures. The model is run on a $16\times8\times8$ replication (2048
spins) of the two-atom Fe primitive cell, with periodic boundaries, a rigid (spin-only) lattice and zero
single-ion anisotropy. Each run starts from the fully aligned FM ground state and is evolved under Langevin
spin dynamics ($dt=0.1$~fs) -- stochastic Landau--Lifshitz--Gilbert dynamics of classical
spins~\cite{gilbert2004,ma2011}, the standard atomistic approach to finite-temperature
magnetism~\cite{skubic2008,evans2014} -- for 200{,}000 steps (20~ps) at twelve temperatures from 10 to 400~K;
the normalized net magnetization $|m|=|\sum_i\hat{s}_i|/N$ is averaged over the second half of each run and
over three independent thermostat seeds per temperature. The Langevin damping is $\alpha=1$: the equilibrium
$|m|(T)$ does not depend on $\alpha$, but the thermalization time of the softest magnons of the finite cell
scales as $1/\alpha$ (see Spin-model validation checks). The independent Metropolis Monte Carlo
runs 2000 equilibration and 2000 measurement sweeps per
temperature on the same 2048-spin lattice.

For mp-1079285 the same machinery carries a three-shell model,
\begin{equation}
H=-\Big(J_b\sum_{\rm chain}+J_{2b}\sum_{\pm2b}+J_\perp\sum_{\rm inter}\Big)\hat{s}_i\!\cdot\!\hat{s}_j
+\sum_{\rm chain}\mathbf{D}\!\cdot\!(\hat{s}_i\times\hat{s}_j),
\end{equation}
on the Fe sublattice (one spin per primitive cell, $|m|=1.68\,\mu_B$ as a classical unit vector). Two
\path{spin/exchange} instances carry the chain bond (cutoff 5~\AA) and the 8.4~\AA\ shell (Bethe--Slater
radial form with its root placed at the chain distance); because the $\pm2b$ bond has the same length as the
inter-chain bonds, spins carry an atom type equal to their chain index modulo four, so that $\pm2b$ pairs
(types differing by two) receive $J_{2b}$ by an explicit per-type override while inter-chain pairs (equal
types) receive $J_\perp$. The DMI is carried by \path{spin/dmi} on the chain bond only (5~\AA\ cutoff), with
its vector calibrated -- not assumed -- by zero-step energy evaluations of the very $90^\circ$ spirals used in
the DFT, so that the model reproduces $E(+)-E(-)$ for both spiral planes. Before any sweep, zero-step
evaluations of the $1\times2\times1$, $1\times4\times1$ ($++--$), $2a$ and $2c$ cells reproduce the four DFT
energy differences -- the two chain cells exactly, the $2a$ and $2c$ cells to $0.5$\%, which is the residual
of fitting one $J_\perp$ to two inequivalent shells. The production cell is an $8\times8\times8$ replication
of the $1\times4\times1$ unit (2048 spins), started from FM along $b$, with the same Langevin protocol at
fourteen temperatures from 5 to 300~K. The same-cell parametrization was swept at 10, 100, 125, 150
and 200~K with three seeds each.

\subsection*{Finite-size scaling}

Sweeps were repeated at $N=256$, 864, 2048 and 4000 spins with the box aspect ratio held fixed, so that every
size sees the same neighbour shells, as 40~ps Langevin runs
($\alpha=1$) with the moments pooled over the last 20~ps of four thermostat seeds; for mp-1079285 the
135--150~K window, where the crossings lie, was run with 10--14 seeds per point, six of them 80~ps. Error bars
on $U_4$ are a drop-one-seed jackknife. $U_4$ of a three-component order parameter tends to $2/3$ in the
ordered phase and $4/9$ in the disordered one and is size-independent at $T_c$. Because $U_4$ is a ratio of
small fluctuating moments, its jackknife error grows from $\pm0.0003$--$0.002$ at 95~K to $\pm0.03$ above $T_c$
for mp-754090 (from $\pm0.0002$--$0.0007$ at 120~K to $\pm0.004$--$0.028$ at 150~K for mp-1079285), and in that
noisy regime the difference between two sizes can change sign on scales far below its own error bar. The
quoted crossings are therefore the lowest-temperature sign changes that persist: the difference is required
to stay positive at a majority of the higher temperatures measured, which discards both the sub-noise flips
inside the ordered plateau, where every $U_4$ has converged on $2/3$, and isolated single-point reversals above
$T_c$; one such non-persistent sign change was discarded for
mp-1079285, whose 155 and 160~K points retain only four seeds each and scatter back above $4/9$. $T_c$ and its
uncertainty are the mean and standard deviation of the three pairwise crossings of consecutive sizes. The
longer runs at 135--150~K cut the $U_4$ error where the crossings lie ($\pm0.048\rightarrow\pm0.014$ for
$N=4000$ at 145~K), though not uniformly: the same cell's error at 150~K grew from $\pm0.020$ to $\pm0.028$ as
the longer runs sampled more of the disordered-phase excursions.

Re-deriving both crossings under changes to the sampling window (Supplementary Note~3) shows that the
half-run equilibration cut is required and that the autocorrelation time of $U_4$ near $T_c$ is comparable to
the run length, so the quoted spreads are lower bounds.

\subsection*{Spin-model validation checks}

Each model passes four checks before any sweep (Supplementary Note~4 describes the errors they caught): a zero-step energy evaluation of the model against every DFT pair it was built
from (for mp-1079285 the $++--$ check is what exposes a $\pm2b$ bond silently given $J_\perp$); an explicit
count and geometry of the neighbour shells the cutoff admits; an independent Metropolis Monte Carlo
calculation of $|m|(T)$ on the same lattice; and a window-drift test on every reported point, comparing the
mean of $|m|$ over consecutive 2~ps windows of the averaged half. For the mp-1079285 production sweep the
seed-averaged drift is $\le0.008$ at every temperature except $+0.018$ at 150~K, against within-run
fluctuations of $\pm0.05$ there, with no monotonic trend; for the same-cell sweep it is $\le0.008$ except
$+0.05$ at 150~K, the transition point, against a within-run fluctuation of $\pm0.04$; for mp-754090 the 100~K point has a within-run fluctuation of $0.043$ and window
means that differ by up to $0.049$.

\section*{Supplementary Information}

\subsection*{Supplementary Note 1: MAGNDATA retrieval}

A new end-to-end scraper against the live Bilbao Crystallographic Server
index recovered 1{,}919 of 2{,}414 entries (79.5\%). The 495 failures break down as 449 HTTP 404s from the
server's temporary \texttt{.mcif} endpoint, 41 structures the parser refuses (36 explicitly disordered, 5
incommensurate), 3 read timeouts, and 2 entries whose fetched CIF contained no structure. Within the 404s, 148
are entries whose formula carries fractional site occupancies and 3 carry HTML markup in the upstream formula
string, which makes the request URL malformed; those 151, together with the 41 refusals, are the at most 192
entries attributable to a data-format limitation. The remaining 298 are 404s on well-formed URLs for
integer-stoichiometry entries, and they are transient rather than permanent: they persist across the
scraper's four in-run retries, but re-requesting one of them (entry 0.57, ScFeO$_3$) returned 404 and then
200 within minutes, while its entry page is served throughout. 79.5\% is therefore a property of this run
rather than a ceiling. Nothing below depends on the missing entries, all of which are simply absent from the
training and validation sets, but the MAGNDATA ground truth used here is smaller than the source allows.

\subsection*{Supplementary Note 2: the JARVIS-DFT \texttt{ehull} field}

The \texttt{ehull} field of the JARVIS-DFT snapshot used here is not a hull distance (its median over the
93{,}902-entry snapshot is 1.7~eV~atom$^{-1}$, the known stable compound TbMnSi carries
\texttt{ehull}$=2.45$~eV~atom$^{-1}$, and 101 of the 3{,}206 harmonized JARVIS records with a \emph{positive}
formation energy carry \texttt{ehull}$=0$, among them BaFeHg$_4$ at $+0.36$~eV~atom$^{-1}$); it was therefore
discarded and the hull distance of every JARVIS record recomputed from JARVIS's own OptB88vdW formation
energies with a per-chemical-system convex hull (13{,}967 of 19{,}116 records within 100~\meVat). Three
of the four JARVIS entries in the original top-30 shortlist fail this recomputed criterion (Mg$_6$FeBi 0.13,
BaFeHg$_4$ 0.36, Ba$_4$ScHg 0.54~eV~atom$^{-1}$); none of them entered the DFT batch, which is Materials
Project only.

\subsection*{Supplementary Note 3: sampling-window robustness of the Binder crossings}

Both crossings were re-derived under changes to the sampling window to test how much of the quoted spread is
statistical. The equilibration cut matters and cannot be relaxed: discarding only the first quarter of each run
instead of the first half moves mp-754090 to $109.8$~K and mp-1079285 to $134.5$~K, because the retained data
still contain the thermalization transient from the ordered start. With the half-discard in place, mp-754090 is
insensitive to further truncation -- averaging only the last quarter gives $98.0/102.4/103.0$~K, mean
$101.1$~K, against the published $101.2$~K. mp-1079285 is not: its last quarter gives $141.0/141.1/123.4$~K,
mean $135.2$~K, and the entire shift comes from the $2048\times4000$ pair. A check that shortens no window --
analyzing only the six 80~ps runs, which cover 135--150~K -- returns $140.0/143.2/135.7$~K, mean $139.6$~K,
within $0.3$~K of the published value. The instability of individual pairs is visible directly in the raw
series: one 40~ps seed at $N=4000$, 140~K has $U_4=0.582$ in its third quarter and $0.383$ in its fourth (a
10~ps window each), so the autocorrelation time of $U_4$ near $T_c$ is comparable to the run length. Pooling
10--14 seeds suppresses the residual quarter-to-quarter difference of the pooled estimate to $\le0.039$ but does
not eliminate it.

\subsection*{Supplementary Note 4: errors caught by the spin-model checks}

The mp-754090 spin model was corrected twice during this work, and the Langevin protocol once; each error was
caught by a check that we now apply to every model before a sweep. A first extraction quoted $J=\Delta E/4$,
double-counting the flipped bonds ($J=12.06$~meV, which placed the $|m|(T)$ knee at 150--200~K). The next used
a 5.25~\AA\ neighbour cutoff that keeps only the six coplanar in-plane bonds and drops the six inter-layer ones,
so that $\Delta E = 8J$ and $J=5.57$~meV -- the correct sum of an in-plane and an inter-layer bond, but on a
lattice of uncoupled two-dimensional triangular layers, which has no long-range order at any finite
temperature; once properly equilibrated it placed the knee at 25--50~K, and the Monte Carlo on that lattice
gives $|m|(10~{\rm K})=0.64$ and $0.23$ at 25~K, the no-long-range-order signature that exposed the cutoff
error. Finally, at the conventional damping $\alpha=0.05$ neither 4~ps nor 20~ps runs had equilibrated at the
temperatures nearest the transition (at 100~K the seed-averaged $|m|$ was still falling across the averaged
half of both: from $0.49$ in its first $0.5$~ps to $0.38$ in its last for the 4~ps run, and from $0.15$ in its
first 2~ps window to $0.07$ in its last for the 20~ps run); those runs are kept
in the repository and approach the reported values
from above.
