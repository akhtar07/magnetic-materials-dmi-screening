\begin{abstract}
High-throughput databases such as the Materials Project (MP) are widely used as the first filter in
computational searches for Dzyaloshinskii--Moriya-interaction (DMI) hosts for chiral spin-texture and
skyrmion-based non-volatile memory. These searches typically trust the database's recorded magnetic
ordering, even though MP's automated relaxation workflow initializes every calculation from a single
ferromagnetic (FM) moment guess and rarely explores antiferromagnetic (AFM) alternatives. We report a
direct density-functional-theory (DFT) test of this assumption on a structural family --
noncentrosymmetric, heavy-spin-orbit-coupling-element, net-moment compounds -- selected because it is
exactly the population that DMI-candidate screening pipelines draw from. Using MP's own energy-mapping
protocol, with documented deviations only where it fails to converge, we enumerate and converge FM and symmetry-distinct AFM orderings for 26
candidates drawn from a machine-learning-filtered shortlist built on a seven-source, 64{,}635-record
harmonized materials database; every calculation passes a per-ion spin-state audit or is re-run, and
marginal cases are re-decided with tight same-cell FM/AFM pairs. Twenty-one of the 26 MP ``ground-truth''
FM labels are overturned by a lower-energy AFM state (margins of 0.19--24.68~\meVat), three are confirmed
FM, and two are degenerate to within electronic-convergence precision. This DFT-level result independently corroborates,
in a materially different and more consequential population, the large-N statistical finding of systematic
FM bias in MP recently reported from MAGNDATA-trained ML classifiers [Fahmy, arXiv:2509.05909].
For one confirmed-FM candidate, Li$_4$Fe$_2$TeWO$_{12}$ (mp-754090), we go one step further and extract
both the isotropic exchange constant ($J=2.78$~meV per Fe--Fe bond on the twelve-neighbor close-packed
Fe sublattice, from a same-cell FM/AFM energy mapping) and one projection of the DMI vector ($D=0.10$~meV,
$D/J\approx3.5\%$, from a constrained noncollinear+spin-orbit canting-angle probe) directly from DFT, and use
the exchange, unmodified, as the input to finite-temperature LAMMPS-SPIN dynamics; for a second confirmed-FM candidate, Tb$_6$FeBi$_2$ (mp-1079285), constrained
spin spirals resolve both symmetry-allowed components of the Fe-chain DMI vector ($|D|=0.10$~meV per bond,
$D/J\approx0.7\%$). Starting from the ordered ground state, the Fe-sublattice magnetization of mp-754090 falls from
$|m|=0.97$ at 10~K to the finite-size floor above 125~K, and a Binder-cumulant crossing over four system
sizes (256--4000 spins) places the ordering temperature of the model at $101\pm1$~K, matching the
classical-Heisenberg fcc estimate ($\approx$103~K) for the same $J$ and falling 22\% below mean field
($\approx$129~K, which therefore overestimates it by 28\%); the curve is reproduced by an independent Monte Carlo calculation. The three-shell model of mp-1079285
($zJ=52.6$~meV) gives a crossing at $139\pm3$~K. Taken together, these results
argue that MP's ordering label should be treated as a screening prior rather than ground truth for exactly
the structural family that DMI-material discovery pipelines target, and they demonstrate what a full,
DFT-grounded verification of one candidate costs and returns.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Materials that host a stable Dzyaloshinskii--Moriya interaction (DMI)~\cite{dzyaloshinsky1958,moriya1960}
are of active interest for chiral spin-texture and skyrmion-based non-volatile memory, because the
antisymmetric exchange term the DMI contributes can stabilize spin spirals and skyrmion lattices at zero
field~\cite{fert2013,nagaosa2013}. Searching for such materials computationally begins, almost universally,
with a curated high-throughput database -- most commonly the Materials Project (MP)~\cite{jain2013} -- used
to filter tens of thousands of candidates down to a tractable shortlist before committing expensive
first-principles or simulation resources. That filtering step depends on the database's recorded magnetic
ordering (ferromagnetic, antiferromagnetic, ferrimagnetic, or non-magnetic) being correct, or at least an
unbiased estimate.

It is well documented that automated high-throughput DFT workflows are prone to a specific failure mode
here: relaxing a structure from a single initial magnetic-moment guess -- almost always ferromagnetic --
converges to whichever local minimum that guess is closest to, without necessarily finding the true magnetic
ground state~\cite{horton2019}. Materials Project's own magnetic-ordering methodology paper is explicit that
a full energy-mapping analysis, enumerating symmetry-distinct antiferromagnetic configurations and
comparing converged energies, is required to determine the ground state
reliably~\cite{horton2019}; the bulk of MP's own entries, generated at scale, do not receive this treatment.

Recent independent evidence, obtained through a very different route, points at the same problem.
Fahmy~\cite{fahmy2026} trained propagation-vector and magnetic-order classifiers on the MAGNDATA database of
experimentally solved magnetic structures~\cite{gallego2016a,gallego2016b} and applied them to the Materials
Project database, finding a systematic disagreement consistent with a ferromagnetic bias affecting more than
7{,}843 MP-labeled compounds. That is a large-N, purely statistical detection: the classifier disagrees with
the label, but no first-principles calculation is performed on any individual compound to confirm which side
is right.

This work asks a complementary question at higher fidelity but lower N: within the specific structural
family that DMI-candidate screening pipelines actually select -- noncentrosymmetric space groups, a
net magnetic moment, and a heavy spin-orbit-coupling element present -- how often is MP's stored FM label
the true DFT ground state? We build a seven-source harmonized materials database and a bias-aware
magnetic-ordering classifier, use them to filter for DMI-compatible candidates, and then run the same
collinear energy-mapping protocol MP itself prescribes~\cite{horton2019} on 26 of the resulting shortlisted
candidates. The result is a direct, per-compound DFT verdict rather than a statistical classifier
disagreement, obtained specifically in the population most consequential for DMI-material discovery. We
then carry one confirmed-FM candidate, Li$_4$Fe$_2$TeWO$_{12}$ (mp-754090), through a full pipeline: DFT
extraction of both the isotropic exchange constant $J$ and the DMI constant $D$, and finite-temperature
atomistic spin dynamics driven by those DFT-derived constants with no fitting or rescaling.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Harmonized multi-source database}

Seven public sources -- Materials Project (37{,}783 records, via \texttt{mp-api}), JARVIS-DFT (19{,}116),
AFLOW (2{,}639), MAGNDATA (1{,}919 of 2{,}414 attempted), 2DMatPedia (1{,}862), OQMD (816), and NOMAD (500) --
were merged into one schema (\texttt{material\_id}, \texttt{formula}, \texttt{elements},
\texttt{space\_group}, \texttt{density}, \texttt{volume}, \texttt{band\_gap}, \texttt{ordering},
\texttt{total\_magnetization}, and, where available, full structure and per-site magnetic-moment vectors),
for 64{,}635 total records. Records retain a source tag rather than being silently deduplicated across
sources. MAGNDATA is the only source with experimentally determined (rather than DFT-derived) magnetic
structure, and is accordingly the ground-truth source for classifier training and validation. A new
end-to-end scraper against the live Bilbao Crystallographic Server index recovered 1{,}919 of 2{,}414
entries (79.5\%). The 495 failures break down as 449 HTTP 404s from the server's temporary
\texttt{.mcif} endpoint, 41 structures the parser refuses (36 explicitly disordered, 5 incommensurate),
3 read timeouts, and 2 entries whose fetched CIF contained no structure. Within the 404s, 148 are entries
whose formula carries fractional site occupancies and 3 carry HTML markup in the upstream formula string,
which makes the request URL malformed; those 151, together with the 41 refusals, are the at most 192
entries attributable to a data-format limitation. The remaining 298 are 404s on well-formed URLs for
integer-stoichiometry entries, and they are transient rather than permanent: they persist across the
scraper's four in-run retries, but re-requesting one of them (entry 0.57, ScFeO$_3$) returned 404 and then
200 within minutes, while its entry page is served throughout. 79.5\% is therefore a property of this run
rather than a ceiling, and a slower retry campaign should recover most of the 298. Nothing below depends on the missing entries, all of which are simply
absent from the training and validation sets, but the MAGNDATA ground truth used here is smaller than the
source allows. Ordering was validated on a known case (LaMnO$_3$, correctly recovered as AFM, space group
$Pnma$).

\subsection{Structure-aware magnetic-ordering classifier}

A LightGBM classifier predicts ordering (FM/AFM/FiM/NM) from composition- and structure-derived features. A
composition-only baseline (per-element statistics of atomic number, mass, electronegativity, atomic radius,
plus group/period/transition-metal/rare-earth fractions) achieves 56.7\% accuracy (60.7\% balanced
accuracy). Adding two tiers of structural features -- broad, near-universal-coverage fields (space group
number, crystal-system bucket, unit-cell volume, density, site count) and local-geometry features around
magnetic sites (nearest magnetic--magnetic neighbor distance, magnetic coordination number, fraction of
magnetic sites; computed from atomic positions and species only, never from the magnetic-moment vectors
that generate the label itself) -- raises this to 67.4\% accuracy (68.0\% balanced accuracy) on a
stratified 28{,}769/7{,}193 train/test split (Fig.~\ref{fig:classifier}). In the first version of this pipeline the local-geometry
tier was computed only for MAGNDATA, whose CIFs carry atomic positions inline; every other source was
represented by its metadata alone. Because MAGNDATA is the one source whose labels are experimental and
83\% AFM, a ``Tier-2 present'' pattern then acted as a proxy for the source database rather than for
geometry. We therefore fetched structures for every source with a public structure endpoint (Materials
Project 37{,}783/37{,}783, JARVIS 19{,}116/19{,}116, AFLOW 2{,}637/2{,}639, NOMAD 495/500, 2DMatPedia
1{,}862/1{,}862; OQMD 0/816 because its REST endpoint was unreachable throughout this revision) and
recomputed the tier for all of them. The change leaves the headline numbers essentially unchanged (67.0\%
$\to$ 67.4\% accuracy, 68.1\% $\to$ 68.0\% balanced) but moves the model's reliance where it belongs:
per-source test accuracy on Materials Project rises from 65.6\% to 66.9\% (balanced 63.0\% $\to$ 65.9\%),
while the inflated MAGNDATA figure drops from 89.5\% to 75.8\%, and four local-geometry features
(mean/std/min magnetic--magnetic distance, mean magnetic coordination) enter the top seven features by
LightGBM split importance, where before none appeared in the top ten (Fig.~\ref{fig:classifier}b). Space-group number remains the single most
important feature, despite being absent from the baseline entirely. This classifier is retrained on all
labeled data and deployed to predict ordering for every record lacking a ground-truth label, including all
19{,}116 JARVIS records, none of which carry a populated ordering field.

\subsection{DMI-compatibility screening funnel}

The full 64{,}635-record database, with ordering back-filled by the deployment classifier where no
ground-truth label exists, is filtered for candidates plausibly compatible with a DMI: a net magnetic
moment (FM or FiM; AFM is excluded, since a texture-hosting net moment is a hard requirement for the
downstream simulation stage), a noncentrosymmetric space group (DMI is symmetry-forbidden under an
inversion center~\cite{moriya1960}; verified computationally against all 230 space groups, recovering the
textbook 92 centrosymmetric / 138 noncentrosymmetric split), presence of at least one heavy
spin-orbit-coupling element (Ta, W, Re, Os, Ir, Pt, Au, Hg, Bi -- the classic DMI-enhancing species by the
Fert--Levy and Moriya mechanisms~\cite{fert1980,moriya1960}), and thermodynamic plausibility
($E_\mathrm{hull} \le 0.1$~eV/atom where known). Of 64{,}635 records, 47{,}614 carry a net-moment
ordering, 19{,}280 of those are noncentrosymmetric, and 1{,}864 also contain a heavy-SOC element
(Materials Project 768, JARVIS 537, AFLOW 392, 2DMatPedia 77, NOMAD 53, OQMD 35, MAGNDATA 2;
Fig.~\ref{fig:funnel}). A hull energy is known for 1{,}340 of them (the MP, JARVIS and OQMD records), 671 of which lie within
0.1~eV/atom of it. Two revisions to this step were made during the present audit. First, the
\texttt{ehull} field of the JARVIS-DFT snapshot used here is not a hull distance (its median over the
93{,}902-entry snapshot is 1.7~eV/atom, the known stable compound TbMnSi carries
\texttt{ehull}$=2.45$~eV/atom, and 101 of the 3{,}206 harmonized JARVIS records with a \emph{positive}
formation energy carry \texttt{ehull}$=0$ --- among them BaFeHg$_4$ at $+0.36$~eV/atom, which is how the
inconsistency was found); it was therefore discarded and the hull distance of every JARVIS record recomputed
from JARVIS's own OptB88vdW formation energies with a per-chemical-system convex hull (13{,}967 of 19{,}116
records within 0.1~eV/atom). Three of the four JARVIS entries in the original top-30 shortlist fail this
recomputed criterion (Mg$_6$FeBi 0.13, BaFeHg$_4$ 0.36, Ba$_4$ScHg 0.54~eV/atom); none of them entered the
DFT batch, which is Materials Project only. Second, the hull criterion is now available as a hard
filter: dropping every record with a \emph{known} hull distance above 0.1~eV/atom (records from sources
that provide no hull energy -- AFLOW, NOMAD, 2DMatPedia, MAGNDATA -- are retained rather than penalised for
a missing field) leaves 1{,}195 candidates. All 26 candidates of the DFT batch survive the hard filter,
and the original ranking from which that batch was drawn, in which the hull acted as a sort key rather than
an exclusion, is kept alongside it so the batch remains reproducible; because the hard-filtered ranking
orders stable records by hull distance first, the same 26 candidates spread from rank 1 to rank 663 under
it, i.e.\ the DFT batch is not a sample of the funnel's most stable members (Fig.~\ref{fig:hull}).

For the top 100 screened candidates per source, structures were backfilled via each source's
own public data-access method (298 of 300 attempted backfills succeeded; two AFLOW records failed on
repeated TLS errors from the AFLUX endpoint) and re-relaxed with a MACE
foundation-model potential~\cite{batatia2022}, fine-tuned on this project's harmonized dataset via multihead
training (a replay head sampling 20{,}000 configurations from the original pretraining distribution to
avoid catastrophic forgetting), as an independent consistency check against each source's own DFT
relaxation. The base model is MACE-MP-0 \texttt{small} (\texttt{num\_channels}$=128$, \texttt{max\_L}$=1$,
two interaction layers, $r_\mathrm{max}=5.0$~\AA); the relaxations use the Stage-2/SWA checkpoint of the
fine-tune with its default head, and are static FIRE optimizations in ASE (no molecular dynamics anywhere
in this work). All 298 relaxations converged (max force $\le 0.05$~eV/\AA) with a median relaxation-energy
shift of 0.0009~eV/atom relative to the source structure, indicating strong agreement between the
fine-tuned potential and each source's own DFT. Classifier confidence and MACE relaxation-consistency were
merged into a final ranking (converged $\to$ hull-stable $\to$ smallest relaxation shift $\to$ classifier
confidence); the resulting top-30 shortlist is the pool this work's DFT validation batch is drawn from.

\subsection{DFT ordering-validation protocol}

For a subset of shortlisted candidates carrying an \texttt{ordering\_source=ground\_truth},
\texttt{confidence=1.0} label from Materials Project (i.e., MP's own recorded ordering, not a
classifier prediction), we apply the same collinear magnetic-ordering energy-mapping approach behind MP's
own workflow~\cite{horton2019}: symmetry-distinct FM and AFM spin configurations are enumerated with
\texttt{pymatgen.MagneticStructureEnumerator} (backed by \texttt{enumlib}); VASP inputs are generated with
\texttt{MPRelaxSet} -- MP's own exchange-correlation functional, GGA+U parameters (e.g. LDAUU$=5.3$~eV on
Fe), plane-wave cutoff (520~eV), and $k$-point convention -- overriding only the initial MAGMOM per
configuration; and each configuration is relaxed to convergence (VASP~6.4.3; \texttt{MPRelaxSet}
defaults ISIF$=3$, ISMEAR$=-5$, EDIFF$=5\times10^{-5}$~eV per atom, i.e.\ 0.05~\meVat). This choice keeps
the resulting energies directly comparable to MP's own stored values rather than constituting an arbitrary
independent DFT recipe. Beyond VASP's own convergence flag, every calculation was audited in two further
ways: the final RMS charge-density residual was checked (the ``EDIFF is reached'' banner can fire on a
transient while the density is still far from self-consistent), and the final per-ion moments were compared
against the intended MAGMOM pattern and against the moment magnitudes of the FM calculation of the same
compound. The second check matters: a configuration whose magnetic ions collapse to a low-spin or
nonmagnetic state, or whose moments re-order into the FM pattern during the SCF cycle (a doubled ``AFM''
cell that is really FM), passes VASP's convergence test but is not a sample of the ordering it was labeled
with, and is excluded from the verdict rather than counted.

The first pass (93 relaxations across the 26 candidates; 116 audited relaxations in total once the
second batch below is included) left 12 calculations in seven candidates that
failed the spin-state audit: collapsed or low-spin Fe/Cr moments in an AFM configuration (mp-1173139,
mp-1173143, mp-551086, mp-775194), ``AFM'' cells whose moments re-ordered to the FM pattern during the SCF
cycle (mp-682554), an ``AFM'' pattern that had flipped only a nonmagnetic $d^0$ Ti site and was therefore FM
in disguise (mp-1216981, whose apparent zero margin in an earlier draft was an artefact of exactly this),
and an FM reference that collapsed to a nonmagnetic state (mp-22972). Rather than discarding these
candidates, a second batch replaced every failed configuration with a freshly enumerated one in a spin state
that the audit accepts: additional symmetry-distinct AFM/FiM cells for mp-1173139, mp-1173143, mp-775194 and
mp-551086 (the ferrimagnetic ones with the two Fe sublattices, or Fe and Cr, unequal in number), Fe-only AFM
supercells for mp-1216981 (Ti excluded from the enumeration), anti-collapse FM reruns for mp-22972 (in the
primitive cell and in the cell of its lowest AFM configuration), and, for mp-682554, whose $1\times1\times2$
Fe--Fe stacking (130 atoms, 27.9~\AA{} between the two Fe sites) diverged twice at the first density-mixing
step through charge sloshing, five single-point mixer/smearing variants of the same cell run side by side,
with the production relaxation restarted only under the first variant that converged. A superseded attempt
(a diverged or collapsed run that was re-done) is neither a sample nor a gap in the count.

Two protocol effects turned out to matter at the sub-meV/atom level. First, the MP recipe compares an FM
relaxation in the primitive cell with AFM relaxations in supercells, each with its own $k$-mesh and each
relaxed with ISIF$=3$ to its own cell shape; for the sixteen configurations run both ways this ``loose''
margin differs from a ``tight'' one (both orderings relaxed with ISIF$=2$ in the same relaxed AFM cell,
identical $k$-mesh, EDIFF$=10^{-6}$~eV, EDIFFG$=-0.01$~eV/\AA) by up to 1.3~\meVat\ in either direction
(Ba$_4$Ta$_{10}$FeO$_{30}$: $+1.18\to0.00$; FeP$_6$(WO$_8$)$_3$: $+2.09\to+3.38$). Every candidate whose
verdict rested on a loose margin smaller than about 2~\meVat\ was therefore re-decided with a tight pair
built in the cell of its lowest valid non-FM configuration (14 pairs in the first tight batch, 6 more for
the remaining small-margin overturns), and a tight number replaces the loose one for that configuration
wherever it exists. Second, the verdict rules are asymmetric by construction: a label is \emph{overturned}
as soon as one audited configuration lies more than 0.10~\meVat\ below FM -- a still-missing configuration
cannot undo that -- whereas \emph{confirmed FM} and \emph{degenerate} require every staged configuration
to have a valid energy. The 0.10~\meVat\ window is twice the per-atom EDIFF of the loose protocol.

All five tungsten-bearing candidates in this batch initially failed to converge under MP's standard Hubbard
correction (LDAUU$=6.2$~eV on W): tungsten here is formally W$^{6+}$, a nominally $d^0$ ion, and a large $U$
applied to a near-empty $d$-shell leaves the self-consistent density with no restoring force, oscillating
indefinitely rather than converging. Removing $U$ from tungsten (LDAUL$=-1$) entirely, applied uniformly
across every ordering of the affected compound, converged all 18 corresponding production calculations
cleanly on the first retry. These reruns also fix the cell shape and volume at MP's relaxed values
(ISIF$=2$, ions only) and use Gaussian smearing (ISMEAR$=0$, $\sigma=0.05$~eV; tetrahedron smearing for
mp-755203) for SCF stability. This changes the absolute energy scale for those five candidates relative to
MP's own U-inclusive value, but not the internal FM-vs-AFM comparison within each compound, since the same
settings are applied to every ordering of that compound identically.

\subsection{Exchange and DMI extraction for mp-754090}

For Li$_4$Fe$_2$TeWO$_{12}$ (mp-754090), confirmed FM by the protocol above, we additionally extract
effective spin-Hamiltonian parameters directly from DFT. The isotropic nearest-shell exchange constant is
obtained from the tight same-cell FM/AFM pair used for the ordering verdict (both orderings relaxed in the
same cell, which is the fixed lattice a Heisenberg $J$ is defined on). The Fe sublattice of the
rock-salt-derived Li$_4$Fe$_2$TeWO$_{12}$ structure is close-packed: each of the two Fe sites (Fe0, Fe1)
per formula-unit cell has twelve Fe neighbors, six in a triangular layer (5.112--5.157~\AA, bond angles
of $60^\circ$, $120^\circ$ and $180^\circ$; four on the opposite sublattice and two on the same sublattice by
lattice translation) and six across the 4.53~\AA{} layer spacing (5.411--5.427~\AA; again four on the
opposite sublattice and two on the same). The next shell starts at 7.45~\AA. For
$H=-\sum_{\langle ij\rangle} J\,\hat{s}_i\!\cdot\!\hat{s}_j$ with each bond counted once, the collinear AFM
configuration (Fe0 sublattice reversed) flips the eight inter-sublattice bonds per cell -- four in-plane and
four inter-layer -- so $E_\mathrm{AFM}-E_\mathrm{FM} = 2\times8\,J = 16J$; with the same-cell DFT value
$\Delta E = 44.54$~meV per two-Fe cell this gives $J = \Delta E/16 = 2.78$~meV per bond. The loose pair
(each ordering at its own ISIF$=3$ optimum) gives $\Delta E = 48.23$~meV and $J=3.01$~meV, an 8\%
protocol spread that we carry as the systematic uncertainty of $J$. (Two earlier versions of this
analysis were wrong in ways worth recording: one quoted $J=\Delta E/4$, double-counting the flipped bonds;
the next used a 5.25~\AA{} neighbor cutoff that keeps only the six coplanar in-plane bonds and drops the
six inter-layer ones, so that $\Delta E = 8J$ and $J=5.57$~meV -- the correct sum of an in-plane and an
inter-layer bond, but on a lattice of \emph{uncoupled} two-dimensional triangular layers, which has no
long-range order at any finite temperature and gave a spurious $|m|(T)$ once the spin dynamics were
properly equilibrated. The spin dynamics reported below use the twelve-neighbor model.) A single FM/AFM
pair cannot separate the in-plane from the inter-layer exchange, nor resolve the four same-sublattice
bonds, which are invisible to a two-configuration fit; all twelve bonds are assigned the same $J$ in the
spin model -- an explicit assumption. This is a single-$J$ effective model, not a multi-shell
decomposition; its $zJ = 12J = 33.4$~meV, which fixes the mean-field ordering temperature, is what the DFT
pair determines directly.

DMI does not appear in a collinear FM-vs-AFM comparison at all; it requires spins canted relative to one
another. We first checked whether mp-754090's crystal symmetry forces $D=0$ via Moriya's rule (an inversion
center at the bond midpoint is sufficient to forbid DMI exactly, at zero DFT cost)~\cite{moriya1960}: the
specific Li/Fe/Te/W cation ordering in this structure reduces the space group to $P1$, with no symmetry
operation beyond the identity, so no such shortcut applies and a real calculation is required. We then ran
two static, constrained noncollinear-spin+spin-orbit-coupling calculations from the relaxed FM structure,
using the penalty-energy method (\texttt{I\_CONSTRAINED\_M=1}, constraining moment direction only) to hold
Fe0 along $+\hat{z}$ and Fe1 along $+\hat{x}$ (canted$_+$) or $-\hat{x}$ (canted$_-$) -- i.e. $\theta=\pm
90^\circ$ canting in the $xz$-plane, an arbitrary but fixed reference axis since $P1$ symmetry does not
privilege any particular plane. The moment constraint held essentially exactly in both calculations (Fe1
converged to $4.038\,\mu_B$ along $x$ in canted$_+$ and $-4.039\,\mu_B$ in canted$_-$; Fe0 leakage off
$z$ was $<0.002\,\mu_B$ in both). With the same bond-counting convention as for $J$ ($z=8$
inter-sublattice bonds per cell, each counted once; the four same-sublattice bonds connect parallel spins
and contribute neither to the $\cos\theta$ nor to the $\sin\theta$ term), $E(\theta) = E_0 - zJ\cos\theta -
zD\sin\theta$, and comparing $\theta=\pm90^\circ$ isolates the antisymmetric term cleanly since
$\cos(\pm90^\circ)=0$:
\begin{equation}
zD = \frac{E(\theta{=}-90^\circ) - E(\theta{=}+90^\circ)}{2},
\end{equation}
giving $E(-90^\circ)-E(+90^\circ)=1.546$~meV per cell and $D = 0.097$~meV per bond, i.e.\ $D/J \approx
3.5\%$. Because $\hat{s}_1=\hat{z}$ and the canting is confined to the $xz$-plane,
$\hat{s}_1\times\hat{s}_2 = (0,\sin\theta,0)$: this probe measures the mean $y$-component of the DMI
vectors of the eight inequivalent inter-sublattice bonds in this lab frame specifically, not a generic
magnitude, and should be read as a resolved DFT number for one projection of $D$ rather than a fully
characterized three-dimensional DMI vector. Since the ratio $D/J$ is the physically meaningful quantity,
it is unchanged by the bond-count correction above.

\subsection{Exchange and DMI extraction for mp-1079285}
\label{sec:1079285methods}

The second confirmed ferromagnet, Tb$_6$FeBi$_2$ (mp-1079285, space group $Pm$, one Fe per
primitive cell), is a different magnetic topology: the Fe atoms form chains along $b$ (Fe--Fe
4.198~\AA), the next Fe--Fe distances being 8.396~\AA{} along $\pm2b$ (chain next-nearest
neighbours) and 8.396--8.407~\AA{} to the six chain neighbours along $\pm a$, $\pm c$, $\pm(a+c)$.
Tb is treated with the $4f$ electrons frozen in the core (\texttt{Tb\_3} PAW), so the only moment
in the model is Fe ($1.68\,\mu_B$ in the FM calculation), an explicit approximation that ignores any
Tb--Fe exchange. Every collinear AFM configuration that the enumeration produced for this candidate
turned out to be the same chain-antiparallel order in a different cell setting, so the tight pair in
the $1\times2\times1$ cell resolves only the intra-chain coupling: with $H=-\sum_{\langle
ij\rangle}J_{ij}\,\hat{s}_i\!\cdot\!\hat{s}_j$ (each bond counted once) it flips two chain bonds per
cell, $E_\mathrm{AFM}-E_\mathrm{FM}=4J_b$. A chain model alone has no order at any $T>0$, so three
further same-cell tight pairs were staged in supercells of the relaxed FM cell (\texttt{ISIF}$=2$,
\texttt{EDIFF}$=10^{-6}$~eV, \texttt{EDIFFG}$=-0.01$~eV/\AA): the $2a$ and $2c$ cells with the two Fe
antiparallel, which flip the $\pm a,\pm(a+c)$ and the $\pm c,\pm(a+c)$ bonds respectively
($\Delta E_{2a}=4(J_a+J_{ac})$, $\Delta E_{2c}=4(J_c+J_{ac})$), and a $1\times4\times1$ cell with the
chain pattern $++--$, which flips two of the four chain bonds and all four $\pm2b$ bonds
($\Delta E_{4b}=4J_b+8J_{2b}$). Two equations do not determine three inter-chain couplings; the
model takes their mean, $J_\perp=(\Delta E_{2a}+\Delta E_{2c})/16$, under the stated assumption
$J_a\approx J_c\approx J_{ac}$ (the three bond lengths agree to 0.01~\AA). The $\pm2b$ bond is
resolved separately because its length coincides with the inter-chain shell, which no radial
exchange function in the spin-dynamics code can separate (Sec.~\ref{sec:spindyn}).

The DMI vector of the chain bond is constrained by symmetry: the $Pm$ mirror plane is normal to $b$
and contains the Fe site, so by Moriya's rules $\mathbf{D}\perp b$ and two projections determine
$|\mathbf{D}|$. Each was obtained from a pair of $90^\circ$ spin spirals in the $1\times4\times1$
cell (four Fe, moments constrained with \texttt{I\_CONSTRAINED\_M}$=1$, $\lambda=10$, spin-orbit
coupling on, \texttt{EDIFF}$=10^{-7}$~eV, single points on the relaxed FM structure), rotating in
the plane normal to $\hat{n}_1\parallel a$ or $\hat{n}_2=\hat{b}\times\hat{n}_1$ with
either chirality. For a $90^\circ$ spiral $\hat{s}_i\times\hat{s}_j=\pm\hat{n}$ on every chain
bond while the symmetric exchange (including $J_{2b}$, whose bonds are antiparallel in both
chiralities) is identical for the two, so $\mathbf{D}\!\cdot\!\hat{n}=[E(+)-E(-)]/8$ per bond. The
constraint penalty energies were $\le1.6\times10^{-9}$~eV, the Fe moments had no component off
their target axes (to the printed $0.001\,\mu_B$) with $|m_\mathrm{Fe}|=1.50\pm0.01\,\mu_B$ in all
four calculations, and the induced Tb moments ($\le0.123\,\mu_B$; Bi $\le0.002\,\mu_B$) rotate with
the spiral, their magnitudes agreeing between chiralities to $0.001\,\mu_B$.

\subsection{Finite-temperature spin dynamics}
\label{sec:spindyn}

The DFT-derived $J=2.78$~meV, with no fitting or rescaling, parameterizes a twelve-neighbor Heisenberg
Hamiltonian for the Fe sublattice of mp-754090 (framework Li/Te/W/O sites carry no spin and are omitted
from the geometry), implemented in LAMMPS-SPIN~\cite{tranchida2018} via the \texttt{spin/exchange} pair
style (which counts each bond once, matching the convention of the DFT extraction) with a 5.5~\AA{} cutoff
that captures all twelve Fe--Fe bonds per site. \texttt{spin/exchange} uses the Bethe--Slater radial form
$J(r)=4a\,(r/d)^2 e^{-(r/d)^2}$ (with its $b$ parameter set to zero); $d=5.274$~\AA{} is solved so that
$J(5.130~\text{\AA})=J(5.418~\text{\AA})$ exactly and $a$ set so that both equal the DFT $J$, which makes
$J(r)$ constant to within 0.06\% over the two shells. A zero-step energy evaluation of the FM and
Fe0-reversed configurations of the model reproduces the DFT $E_\mathrm{AFM}-E_\mathrm{FM}=16J=44.54$~meV
per cell to 0.003\%. The DMI is not included in the production sweep, for a reason that is itself a
result: the LAMMPS \texttt{spin/dmi} pair style implements $\mathbf{D}_{ij} = D\,(\hat{e}_{ij}\times\hat{v})$
with a single global vector $\hat{v}$, and for the DFT probe geometry ($\hat{s}_i\times\hat{s}_j=\pm\hat{y}$)
its energy difference is $D\,(\hat{v}\times\hat{y})\cdot\sum_b\hat{e}_b$ over the eight inter-sublattice bond
directions -- a sum that is 0.03 (in units of a bond vector) for this near-centrosymmetric neighbor shell,
so no choice of $\hat{v}$ can reproduce the DFT $E(-90^\circ)-E(+90^\circ)$ (a zero-step evaluation with
$D=0.097$~meV and $\hat{v}=\hat{y}$ gives $0.000$~meV); the eight bond-resolved DMI vectors that a faithful
model needs would require at least eight independent canting probes, which are outside the scope of this
work (Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations}). We instead bound the effect of a DMI of the measured magnitude on
$|m|(T)$ with a control sweep that adds \texttt{spin/dmi} with $D=0.097$~meV and $\hat{v}=\hat{y}$ (an
arbitrary but representative placement of a 3.5\% antisymmetric term) at four temperatures, and report the
difference. The model is run on a $16\times8\times8$ replication (2048 spins) of the two-atom Fe primitive
cell, with periodic boundaries and a rigid (spin-only) lattice. Single-ion anisotropy is set to zero
(Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations}). Each run starts from the fully aligned FM ground state and is evolved
under Langevin spin dynamics ($dt=0.1$~fs) for 200{,}000 steps (20~ps) at twelve temperatures from 10 to
400~K; the normalized net magnetization $|m|=|\sum_i\hat{s}_i|/N$ is averaged over the second half of
each run and over three independent thermostat seeds per temperature. The Langevin damping is set to
$\alpha=1$: the equilibrium $|m|(T)$ does not depend on $\alpha$, but the thermalization time of the
softest magnons of the finite cell scales as $1/\alpha$, and at the conventional $\alpha=0.05$ neither
4~ps nor 20~ps runs had equilibrated at the temperatures nearest the transition (at 100~K the
seed-averaged $|m|$ was still falling across the averaged half of both: from $0.49$ in its first $0.5$~ps
to $0.38$ in its last for the 4~ps run, and from $0.15$ in its first 2~ps window to $0.07$ in its last for
the 20~ps run). Equilibration is checked explicitly for every reported point by comparing the
mean of $|m|$ over consecutive 2~ps windows of the averaged half; the $\alpha=0.05$ runs are kept
alongside (\texttt{src/spin\_dynamics/logs\_v3\_4ps}, \texttt{logs\_v3\_a005\_20ps}) and approach the
reported values from above.

For mp-1079285 the same machinery carries a three-shell model, $H=-J_b\sum_{\rm chain}-J_{2b}\sum_{\pm2b}
-J_\perp\sum_{\rm inter}\hat{s}_i\!\cdot\!\hat{s}_j+\sum_{\rm chain}\mathbf{D}\!\cdot\!(\hat{s}_i\times\hat{s}_j)$
on the Fe sublattice (one spin per primitive cell, $|m|=1.68\,\mu_B$ as a classical unit vector).
Two \texttt{spin/exchange} instances carry the chain bond (cutoff 5~\AA) and the 8.4~\AA{} shell
(Bethe--Slater radial form with its root placed at the chain distance); because the $\pm2b$ bond has
the same length as the inter-chain bonds, spins carry an atom type equal to their chain index modulo
four, so that $\pm2b$ pairs (types differing by two) receive $J_{2b}$ by an explicit per-type override
while inter-chain pairs (equal types) receive $J_\perp$. The DMI is carried by \texttt{spin/dmi} on
the chain bond only (5~\AA{} cutoff), with its vector calibrated -- not assumed -- by zero-step
energy evaluations of the very $90^\circ$ spirals used in the DFT, so that the model reproduces
$E(+)-E(-)$ for both spiral planes. Before any sweep, zero-step evaluations of the $1\times2\times1$,
$1\times4\times1$ ($++--$), $2a$ and $2c$ cells reproduce the four DFT energy differences --- the two
chain cells exactly, the $2a$ and $2c$ cells to $0.5$\%, which is the residual of fitting one $J_\perp$ to
two inequivalent shells --- and the $++--$ check is what exposes a $\pm2b$ bond silently given $J_\perp$. The production cell is an
$8\times8\times8$ replication of the $1\times4\times1$ unit (2048 spins), started from FM along
$b$, with the same Langevin protocol as above at fourteen temperatures from 5 to 300~K
(\texttt{src/spin\_dynamics/build\_mp1079285\_model.py}, \texttt{outputs/mp1079285\_spin\_model.json}).

family is more AFM-prone than the ground-truth population as a whole. What it establishes unambiguously is
that MP's FM label cannot be taken at face value for candidates drawn from exactly this population, which
is precisely the population that DMI/skyrmion-material screening pipelines -- including the one used to
generate this shortlist -- select from by construction.

\subsection{mp-754090: DFT-derived exchange, DMI, and finite-temperature ordering}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_mp754090_mvt.pdf}
\caption{Normalized net magnetization of the mp-754090 Fe sublattice (2048-spin cell) versus temperature,
driven purely by the DFT-derived twelve-neighbor $J=2.78$~meV with no fitting; 20~ps Langevin dynamics
($\alpha=1$), mean over three thermostat seeds (error bars: seed-to-seed standard deviation, smaller than
the symbols except at 100~K). Runs start from the ordered state, so the absolute scale is physical
($|m|\to1$ as $T\to0$); the high-temperature floor of $\approx0.025$ is the $1/\sqrt{N}$ finite-size value
of a 2048-spin paramagnet.}
\label{fig:mvt}
\end{figure}

For mp-754090, confirmed FM by 2.23~\meVat, the DFT-derived exchange and DMI constants are $J=2.78$~meV
and $D=0.097$~meV per bond on the twelve-neighbor Fe sublattice, giving $D/J \approx 3.5\%$. This is notably smaller than an earlier
order-of-magnitude placeholder estimate used before this calculation ($D/J\sim25\%$), and is physically
plausible: the
magnetic ion at the probed bond, Fe, is a comparatively light $3d$ species with weak intrinsic spin-orbit
coupling, despite the compound containing tungsten, a heavy-SOC element that is precisely what qualified
this candidate for the DMI-compatibility funnel in the first place. This is a useful cautionary note for the
screening heuristic itself: the presence of a heavy-SOC element anywhere in the composition does not
guarantee a large DMI constant at the specific magnetic-ion bond that anchors the spin texture.

Table~\ref{tab:mvt} and Fig.~\ref{fig:mvt} show the resulting finite-temperature magnetization sweep. $|m|$
decreases monotonically from 0.966 at 10~K through 0.805 at 50~K and 0.659 at 75~K to 0.343 at 100~K,
collapses to 0.068 at 125~K, and then sits on the finite-size floor ($0.025$--$0.044$ for 150--400~K; the
$1/\sqrt{N}$ value for $N=2048$ is 0.022). The transition of the model therefore lies between 100 and
125~K for this cell size, with 100~K -- the only point with an appreciable seed-to-seed spread
($\pm0.027$) and within-run fluctuation ($0.043$ standard deviation over the averaged half, and up to
$0.049$ between its 2~ps window means) -- just below it; the
finite-size-scaling analysis below sharpens this to a single temperature. Two zero-parameter estimates for the same
Hamiltonian bracket this: the mean-field value $T_c^{\rm MF} = zJ/3k_B = 129$~K ($z=12$) and the
classical-Heisenberg fcc value $k_BT_c \approx 3.18\,J$~\cite{chen1993}, i.e.\ $\approx103$~K, which is the
appropriate comparison for a twelve-coordinated close-packed classical unit-spin model. An independent
Metropolis Monte Carlo calculation of the same Hamiltonian on the same 2048-spin lattice
(\texttt{src/spin\_dynamics/mc\_check\_mp754090.py}; 2000 equilibration $+$ 2000 measurement sweeps)
gives $|m| = 0.967$, $0.912$, $0.806$, $0.661$, $0.37\pm0.03$ and $0.07\pm0.02$ at 10, 25, 50, 75, 100 and
125~K, i.e.\ agrees with the Langevin sweep to within 0.002 below 100~K and within the seed spread at
100~K, so the reported curve is the equilibrium $|m|(T)$ of the model and not an artifact of the
thermostat. The same Monte Carlo on the earlier six-neighbor lattice gives $|m|(10~{\rm K})=0.64$ and
$0.23$ at 25~K -- the no-long-range-order signature that exposed the cutoff error. The DMI control sweep
(Sec.~\ref{sec:spindyn}) changes $|m|$ by at most $0.013$ (at 100~K, within the seed spread) and by
$\le0.005$ elsewhere, so an antisymmetric term of the measured 3.5\% magnitude does not move the ordering
scale of this model. The simulated curve is therefore quantitatively consistent with its own inputs;
whether it is consistent with the real material depends on the single-$J$, zero-anisotropy,
rigid-lattice approximations listed in Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations}. For the record, the earlier versions
of this sweep placed the knee at 150--200~K with the double-counted $J=12.06$~meV and, once properly
equilibrated, at 25--50~K with the six-neighbor (layered) model -- both wrong for the reasons given in
Sec.~\ref{sec:spindyn}; the 8\% loose/tight protocol spread in $J$ shifts the transition by about 8~K,
less than the temperature grid spacing.

A finite-size-scaling analysis pins this transition temperature down instead of bracketing it. Repeating
the sweep at $N=256$, 864, 2048 and 4000 spins with the box aspect ratio held fixed (so that every size
sees the same twelve-neighbor shell; \texttt{src/spin\_dynamics/run\_fss.sh}) and forming the
fourth-order Binder cumulant $U_4 = 1-\langle m^4\rangle/3\langle m^2\rangle^2$ of the three-component
order parameter, which tends to $2/3$ in the ordered phase and $4/9$ in the disordered one and is
size-independent at $T_c$, the $U_4(T)$ curves of consecutive sizes cross at $99.5$, $101.3$ and

those corrections) at $102.7$~K. The susceptibility

scales far below its own error bar. The crossings quoted here are therefore the lowest-temperature sign
changes that persist: the difference is required to stay positive at a majority of the higher
temperatures measured, which discards both the sub-noise flips inside the ordered plateau, where every
$U_4$ has converged on $2/3$, and isolated single-point reversals above $T_c$, without asking any one
noisy difference to carry the result (\texttt{src/spin\_dynamics/analyze\_fss.py}).

\subsection{mp-1079285: exchange hierarchy and DMI}
\label{sec:1079285results}

The same-cell pairs of Sec.~\ref{sec:1079285methods} give, for Tb$_6$FeBi$_2$, a strongly anisotropic
exchange network. The chain pair (18-atom $1\times2\times1$ cell) places the chain-antiparallel state
$58.43$~meV per cell above FM, i.e.\ $J_b=14.61$~meV for the 4.198~\AA{} Fe--Fe bond. The two inter-chain
pairs are nearly identical, $\Delta E_{2a}=22.48$ and $\Delta E_{2c}=22.24$~meV per 18-atom cell
(1.25 and 1.24~\meVat; Fe $\pm1.64$--$1.67\,\mu_B$, induced Tb $\le0.21\,\mu_B$ in all four
calculations), which supports the $J_a\approx J_c$ part of the mean-field assumption and gives
$J_\perp=2.80$~meV -- a factor of 5.2 weaker than the chain coupling, but ferromagnetic, so the material
is a three-dimensional ferromagnet of coupled FM chains rather than a quasi-one-dimensional one. The
spiral pairs give $\mathbf{D}\!\cdot\!\hat{n}_1=-0.074$ and $\mathbf{D}\!\cdot\!\hat{n}_2=-0.063$~meV,
$|\mathbf{D}|=0.098$~meV per chain bond and $|\mathbf{D}|/J_b=0.67\%$ -- five times smaller than the
$D/J$ of mp-754090, again with Fe as the only magnetic ion and the heavy elements (Tb, Bi) contributing
only through the ligand field. The $1\times4\times1$ $++--$ pair places that state $84.92$~meV per
36-atom cell above FM; subtracting $4J_b$ from the chain pair leaves $8J_{2b}=26.50$~meV, i.e.\
$J_{2b}=3.31$~meV -- ferromagnetic and comparable to $J_\perp$, so the $\pm2b$ bond is not a
negligible correction: it carries 13\% of $zJ = 2J_b+2J_{2b}+6J_\perp = 52.6$~meV and raises the
mean-field estimate from $T_c^{\rm MF}=178$~K (chain $+$ inter-chain only) to $203$~K. This value is a
difference of two energies computed with different $k$-meshes ($3\times3\times3$ on the
$1\times2\times1$ cell for $J_b$, $3\times2\times3$ on the $1\times4\times1$ cell for $\Delta E_{4b}$).
A same-cell check -- the $+-+-$ chain pattern in the $1\times4\times1$ cell, which flips all four chain
bonds ($\Delta E=8J_b$) and is compared with the FM member of the $++--$ pair -- gives
$\Delta E=100.93$~meV per 36-atom cell, i.e.\ $J_b=12.62$~meV, 14\% below the $1\times2\times1$
value (this relaxation was cut off at ionic step 24 with a maximum force of 0.015~eV/\AA\ and its energy
stationary to $10^{-6}$~eV, so the number is provisional in the same sense as the mp-682554 pair). The
two pairs differ in cell and mesh -- the $1\times2\times1$ pair sits in the relaxed cell of the
enumerated AFM configuration ($a=8.372$, $c=8.376$~\AA) with a mesh equivalent to 6 points along $b$ in
the primitive cell, the $1\times4\times1$ pairs in the relaxed FM cell ($a=8.407$, $c=8.396$~\AA) with
8 -- so the spread is the cell-shape and $k$-convergence sensitivity of a metallic Fe chain, and it
brackets the exchange model: with the same-cell $J_b$ the residual is
$J_{2b}=4.31$~meV, $zJ=50.6$~meV and $T_c^{\rm MF}=196$~K, against $J_{2b}=3.31$, $zJ=52.6$~meV and
$203$~K with the chain-pair value; $|\mathbf{D}|/J_b$ ranges over $0.67$--$0.78\%$. The sweep below uses
the chain-pair parameters (both pairs of that model are force-converged); the same-cell model was swept
at 10, 100, 125, 150 and 200~K to check whether the 4\% change in $zJ$ moves the ordering bracket ---
it does not: $|m|=0.975$, $0.668$, $0.497$, $0.108$ and $0.046$ (three seeds each, window drift
$\le0.008$) against $0.975$, $0.672$, $0.509$, $0.128$ and $0.048$ for the chain-pair model at the same
five temperatures, so the collapse sits between 125 and 150~K in both parametrizations.

Table~\ref{tab:mvt1079285} and Fig.~\ref{fig:mvt1079285} show the finite-temperature sweep of the
three-shell model. $|m|$ falls smoothly from 0.988 at 5~K to 0.672 at 100~K and 0.509 at 125~K, then
collapses to 0.128 at 150~K (the one point with a seed-to-seed spread, $\pm0.012$, and a within-run
fluctuation of $\pm0.05$, as expected at the transition of a finite cell) and sits on the finite-size
floor of 0.03--0.05 from 200~K. The ordering temperature of this cell size is therefore
125--150~K --- the finite-size-scaling analysis below sharpens it to a single temperature --- i.e.\
$0.6$--$0.75\,T_c^{\rm MF}$, the usual reduction of the classical Heisenberg transition below mean
field (for comparison the ratio is 0.72 for the simple-cubic lattice and 0.79 for the
twelve-coordinated fcc one~\cite{chen1993}; this $Pm$ structure has $z=2+2+6$ with unequal couplings,
whose anisotropy is expected to push the ratio below both isotropic values). Every point passed the
equilibration check: the seed-averaged change of $|m|$ between the first and last 2~ps windows of the
averaged half is $\le0.008$ at every temperature (and $+0.018$ at 150~K, against within-window
fluctuations of $\pm0.05$ there), with no monotonic trend.
The sweep is insensitive to the DMI at this $D/J$: $|\mathbf{D}|/J_b=0.67\%$ produces a canting angle of
order $D/2J\approx0.2^\circ$ per bond, far below the thermal disorder at any temperature on the grid.

The same finite-size-scaling analysis was run on this model (Fig.~\ref{fig:fss1079285},
\texttt{outputs/mp1079285\_fss.csv}). The $U_4(T)$ curves of consecutive sizes cross at $140.9$,
$141.4$ and $135.6$~K, giving $T_c = 139.3\pm2.6$~K, and $\chi$ peaks at 145~K for the three smaller
cells and 140~K for the largest. This falls inside the 125--150~K bracket read off the 2048-spin sweep
and puts the transition at $0.68\,T_c^{\rm MF}$, just below the isotropic simple-cubic ratio $0.72$,
as the exchange anisotropy leads one to expect. The crossings are not monotonic in system size as they are for mp-754090,
and the analysis is harder here for two reasons. The exchange is strongly anisotropic --- $J_b/J_\perp
= 5.2$, though ferromagnetic on every shell --- so strong intra-chain correlations survive well above the three-dimensional ordering
temperature and the $U_4$ curves separate gradually rather than pivoting about a point; and the
fluctuations near $T_c$ are correspondingly larger, with the jackknife error on $U_4$ growing from
$\pm0.0002$--$0.0007$ at 120~K to $\pm0.004$--$0.028$ at 150~K. The 135--150~K window was therefore run at 80~ps with
10--14 seeds per point instead of 40~ps with four, which cut the error where the crossings lie
($\pm0.048\rightarrow\pm0.014$ for $N=4000$ at 145~K, though not uniformly: the same cell's error at
150~K grew from $\pm0.020$ to $\pm0.028$ as the longer runs sampled more of the disordered-phase
excursions) and is what makes the three crossings agree to $\pm2.6$~K; the 155 and 160~K points retain only four seeds each and scatter back above the
disordered limit $4/9$, and one such non-persistent sign change was discarded by the rule described
above. The number to quote for this compound is therefore a transition near 140~K with a spread of a
few kelvin, against 125--150~K from the single-cell sweep alone.

Both crossings were re-derived under changes to the sampling window to test how much of the quoted
spread is statistical. The equilibration cut matters and cannot be relaxed: discarding only the first
quarter of each run instead of the first half moves mp-754090 to $109.8$~K and mp-1079285 to
$134.5$~K, because the retained data still contain the thermalization transient from the ordered
start. With the half-discard in place, mp-754090 is insensitive to further truncation --- averaging
only the last quarter gives $98.0/102.4/103.0$~K, mean $101.1$~K, against the published $101.2$~K.
mp-1079285 is not: its last quarter gives $141.0/141.1/123.4$~K, mean $135.2$~K, and the entire shift
comes from the $2048\times4000$ pair. A check that shortens no window --- analyzing only the six 80~ps
runs, which cover 135--150~K --- returns $140.0/143.2/135.7$~K, mean $139.6$~K, within $0.3$~K of the
published value. The instability of individual pairs is visible directly in the raw series: one 40~ps
seed at $N=4000$, 140~K has $U_4=0.582$ in its third quarter and $0.383$ in its fourth (a 10~ps
window each), so the
autocorrelation time of $U_4$ near $T_c$ is comparable to the run length. Pooling 10--14 seeds
suppresses the residual quarter-to-quarter difference of the pooled estimate to $\le0.039$ but does
not eliminate it. The $\pm1.3$ and $\pm2.6$~K spreads over pairwise crossings should therefore be read
as lower bounds on the uncertainty of the model $T_c$, before the DFT coupling uncertainties of
Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations} are added.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Two independent routes to the same conclusion}

Fahmy~\cite{fahmy2026} detects the Materials Project's ferromagnetic bias statistically: a classifier
trained on experimentally solved magnetic structures (MAGNDATA) disagrees with MP's stored label for
thousands of compounds, at large $N$ but without a first-principles check on any individual case. The
present work detects the same qualitative phenomenon by the opposite route -- a small-N, high-fidelity DFT
energy-mapping check, applied specifically within the noncentrosymmetric, heavy-SOC-element, net-moment
structural family that DMI/skyrmion-material discovery pipelines target. That these two very different
methodologies, applied to different (though overlapping-in-spirit) populations, converge on the same
conclusion -- MP's single-initial-guess relaxation workflow systematically under-counts antiferromagnetic
ground states -- is stronger evidence than either result alone. It also sharpens the practical implication
specifically for DMI-material discovery: the population this work checked is not a random cross-section of
MP but exactly the slice that such pipelines filter down to, and a 21-of-26 overturn rate in that slice is a
direct warning to any workflow, including our own, that uses MP's ordering label as a hard screening
criterion rather than a prior.

\subsection{Practical implication for DMI-candidate screening}

The screening funnel used to generate this shortlist (Sec.~II.C) excludes AFM-labeled candidates from
consideration entirely, on the reasoning that a net moment is required for the texture-hosting mechanisms
this project targets. If 21 of 26 top-ranked, MP-labeled-FM candidates from that funnel are, in fact, AFM or ferrimagnetic,
a nontrivial fraction of the true magnetic-material landscape relevant to this search is being filtered out
before any DFT or simulation resource is spent on it -- and, conversely, downstream compute (structure
backfill, MLIP relaxation, spin-dynamics simulation) risks being spent on candidates whose net-moment
premise is simply wrong. The corrective is not to trust the classifier over MP either -- both are, in the
end, estimates -- but to treat any \texttt{ordering} field, ground-truth-labeled or classifier-predicted, as
a prior to be checked with real DFT before committing expensive downstream stages, exactly as done here for
mp-754090.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The 26-candidate validation batch is drawn from the top of one DMI-filtered shortlist, not a random or
stratified sample of the broader \texttt{ground\_truth} population (1{,}864 candidates pass the DMI funnel,
1{,}195 with the hull as a hard filter; 26 of those, not 26 of the full database, were checked). The
mislabeling rate reported here is a property of this structural family, not a project-wide estimate. The
verdict rule is deliberately asymmetric: a candidate is called overturned as soon as one competing
configuration lies below FM by more than 0.10~\meVat, whereas ``confirmed'' requires every staged
configuration to have passed the spin-state audit. The margins of the 12 overturned candidates decided on
a loose (ISIF=3, relaxed-lattice) pair alone carry a protocol uncertainty that the sixteen doubly-computed
configurations bound at 1.3~\meVat. Only one of them has $|$margin$|<2$~\meVat\ (Li$_4$NbFe(WO$_6$)$_2$,
$-0.54$), and it already has a tight pair on a second configuration that corroborates the sign
($-0.47$~\meVat). The six sub-2~\meVat\ overturns re-decided with tight pairs all kept their sign, with
margins moving by at most 0.32~\meVat\ (Sec.~\ref{sec:dftresults}); the mp-682554 pair is force-unconverged
(provisional) as stated there.

The mp-754090 DMI probe resolves one projection (the mean $D_y$ of the eight inequivalent
inter-sublattice bonds, in an arbitrary lab frame fixed consistently across the two canted calculations)
of what is, in $P1$ symmetry, eight unconstrained three-component DMI vectors; a complete characterization
would require at least eight independent canting probes, and without it the DMI cannot be placed in the
spin model in a way that is calibrated against DFT (Sec.~\ref{sec:spindyn}), so the mp-754090 sweep is
exchange-only with the DMI effect bounded by a control. For mp-1079285 the symmetry is high enough
($Pm$) that two spiral pairs fix the full chain-bond vector, which is why its model does carry a
calibrated DMI. The third confirmed ferromagnet, FeP$_6$(WO$_8$)$_3$ (mp-850225), has one Fe per
rhombohedral cell, so its Fe sublattice is a Bravais lattice on which a two-sublattice canting probe
sees no DMI at all (the $+\mathbf{t}$ and $-\mathbf{t}$ bonds contribute opposite
$\mathbf{D}\cdot(\mathbf{S}_0\times\mathbf{S}_1)$); resolving its $D$ needs a spiral in the 102-atom
hexagonal cell, which was designed but not run within this project's compute budget, and its two
exchange shells (six in-plane 8.77~\AA{} and six out-of-plane 8.91~\AA{} bonds, $J_1+J_2=28.7$~meV from
the same-cell pair) were likewise left unseparated. Single-ion anisotropy was not probed in any
compound and is set to zero in the spin dynamics, for lack of any DFT or literature basis for a value.

The magnetization sweeps are classical, unit-spin, rigid-lattice models with zero anisotropy. For
mp-754090 the model is single-$J$: the in-plane and inter-layer bonds, and the four same-sublattice bonds,
are all assigned the one $J$ that a single FM/AFM pair determines; the Fe moment ($\approx4\,\mu_B$ in
DFT) is treated as a classical unit vector. For mp-1079285 the six inter-chain bonds share one
$J_\perp$ by assumption. The Binder-cumulant crossings ($T_c=101.2\pm1.3$~K for
mp-754090, $139.3\pm2.6$~K for mp-1079285) are critical temperatures of the spin models, not of the
materials: they inherit the uncertainty of the DFT-derived couplings (an 8\% loose/tight spread in $J$
for mp-754090, a 14\% spread in $J_b$ for mp-1079285) on top of the classical-unit-vector treatment of
the moment, and the quoted error is only the spread over the three pairwise crossings. The quoted
uncertainties are also unequally supported: the mp-754090 crossings are monotonic in system size, while
those of the chain-anisotropic mp-1079285 model are not, and its 155--160~K points rest on four
seeds each. Finally, the mp-754090 spin model was corrected twice during this work -- a
bond-double-counting error that inflated $J$ by a factor of two, then a neighbor cutoff that silently
discarded the six inter-layer bonds and left a lattice of uncoupled two-dimensional layers -- and the
Langevin sweeps had to be moved from $\alpha=0.05$ to $\alpha=1$ before they were equilibrated. Each of
these was caught by a check that we now regard as mandatory: a zero-step energy evaluation against every
DFT pair, an explicit count and geometry of the neighbor shells the cutoff admits, an independent
Metropolis Monte Carlo calculation of $|m|(T)$ on the same lattice, and a window-drift test on every
reported point.

\section{Conclusion}

Within the specific structural family that Dzyaloshinskii--Moriya-candidate screening pipelines select --
noncentrosymmetric, net-moment, heavy-spin-orbit-coupling-element compounds -- direct DFT energy-mapping on
26 Materials-Project-labeled ferromagnets finds 21 mislabeled (true ground state antiferromagnetic or
ferrimagnetic), 3 confirmed, and 2 degenerate within 0.10~\meVat. This DFT-level result independently corroborates, in a smaller but more
directly consequential population, the large-scale statistical finding of systematic ferromagnetic bias in
MP recently reported from ML classifiers trained on experimentally solved magnetic
structures~\cite{fahmy2026}. For one confirmed candidate, mp-754090, we additionally demonstrate a complete
DFT-to-dynamics pipeline: exchange and DMI constants extracted from first principles, with no fitting, drive
a finite-temperature spin-dynamics simulation whose Binder-cumulant crossing over four system sizes
places the transition at $101\pm1$~K, matching the classical-Heisenberg estimate for the same exchange
constant and an independent Monte Carlo calculation; for a second confirmed candidate, mp-1079285, a
three-shell model with a symmetry-resolved, DFT-calibrated chain DMI orders at $139\pm3$~K. Both results argue for treating high-throughput
database ordering labels as a screening prior rather than ground truth, particularly within the structural
families that downstream physical mechanisms (here, DMI) specifically select for.
